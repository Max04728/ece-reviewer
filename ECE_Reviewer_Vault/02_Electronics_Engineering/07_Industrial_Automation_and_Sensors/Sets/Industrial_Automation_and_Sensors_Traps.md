### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Believing an R-2R DAC is monotonic in every implementation.** Monotonicity requires DNL above $-1$ LSB, which ideal switches give but real switch-resistance mismatch, op-amp offset and reference drift can take away.

<sub>from ECE-07-14</sub>

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reducing PWM ripple without checking settling.** Ripple falls as $1/RC$ but settling grows as $5RC$, so a filter quiet enough for a precision setpoint can take 135 ms to settle and cannot follow any changing waveform.

<sub>from ECE-07-14</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting that ×4 quadrature multiplies the count rate too.** Four times the resolution means four times the edge frequency; at high speed the counter's maximum input frequency, not the encoder, becomes the limit.

<sub>from ECE-07-11</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Placing the stop contact in parallel with the start contact.** The seal-in branch then keeps the output energized through the stop button and the circuit cannot be turned off; the stop must be in series with the whole latch ($Q = (\mathrm{Start}+Q)\overline{\mathrm{Stop}}$).

<sub>from ECE-07-16</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming a difference amplifier is matched because the schematic shows equal values.** The gain $R_2/R_1$ requires $R_2/R_4 = R_1/R_3$ exactly; a single 1% resistor breaks the cancellation and lets the common-mode pedestal through.

<sub>from ECE-07-03</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating the LVDT null as an exact zero.** A real LVDT has a residual null voltage of a few millivolts from winding asymmetry and harmonics, so using the null as a precise position trigger gives a deadband and a repeatability error.

<sub>from ECE-07-11</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Building the ideal integrator with no $R_f$.** Its DC gain is infinite, so a $2\ \mathrm{mV}$ offset or $80\ \mathrm{nA}$ of bias current ramps the output into the rail within seconds. Every practical integrator has a feedback resistor or a reset switch.

<sub>from ECE-07-04</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Equating resolution with accuracy.** Offset, gain error, INL/DNL, reference drift and noise all degrade the real performance: a 16-bit part with 4 LSB of INL or 68 dB of SNR delivers 11-14 effective bits.

<sub>from ECE-07-13</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Charging the inverting configuration the signal gain in the bandwidth formula.** The noise gain is $1+R_f/R_1$, so an inverting $\times 1$ stage has NG = 2 and only half the GBW-limited bandwidth.

<sub>from ECE-07-02</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Blaming the op-amp for poor CMRR.** In a four-resistor difference amplifier the resistor tolerance dominates: 120 dB of op-amp CMRR inside 1% resistors still yields only about 48-68 dB, and no op-amp upgrade fixes it.

<sub>from ECE-07-03</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Trusting the ratio formula beyond the rails.** A $\times 100$ stage on a $120\ \mathrm{mV}$ input on $\pm15\ \mathrm{V}$ rails computes 12 V and delivers a clipped, distorted output; the formula is only valid while the output is inside the rails.

<sub>from ECE-07-02</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reading the Bode asymptotes at the corner.** The asymptotes cross at $f_c$ but the real response is $-3\ \mathrm{dB}$ there for a first-order section and depends on Q for a second-order one.
