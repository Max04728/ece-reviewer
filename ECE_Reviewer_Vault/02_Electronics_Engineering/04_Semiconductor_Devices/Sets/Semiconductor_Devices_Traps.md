### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing degrees and radians in the firing-angle formulas.** $\alpha$ must be in radians in $I_{avg}=\frac{I_p}{2\pi}(1+\cos\alpha)$ but is usually quoted in degrees. A 60 degree firing angle is $\pi/3$, and using 60 in the cosine gives an entirely different current.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing the half-wave and full-wave DC constants.** $V_{dc} = 0.318V_m$ for half-wave and $0.636V_m$ for full-wave. Choosing the wrong one is a factor-of-2 error that still 'looks reasonable' on the page.

<sub>from ECE-04-05</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as the same number.** $\alpha<1$ always (0.95 to 0.998) while $\beta$ is 20 to 500. A common wrong answer is $\alpha=\beta=100$, which violates $I_E=I_C+I_B$.

<sub>from ECE-04-10</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forward-biasing a varactor.** The capacitance formula holds only for $V_R\ge0$. A forward-biased varactor conducts, its $Q$ collapses to that of a ordinary diode, and in a tuned circuit it rectifies the RF and shifts its own bias point. Tuning lines therefore use a blocking capacitor and an RF choke.

<sub>from ECE-04-15</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing a clipper with a clamper.** A clipper changes the peak-to-peak value (it flattens a peak), while a clamper leaves peak-to-peak exactly the same and only moves the dc level. Any answer in which a clamper alters the amplitude, or a clipper leaves it untouched, has the two circuits swapped.

<sub>from ECE-04-07</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Interchanging $R_{dc} = V_D/I_D$ and $r_d = nV_T/I_D$.** For a forward-biased diode they differ by the factor $V_D/(nV_T) \approx 20$ to 40. Using $R_{dc}$ in an ac analysis inflates the answer by that factor; using $r_d$ in a bias calculation does the reverse.

<sub>from ECE-04-03</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating mobility as a constant.** The 1350/480 figures are low-doping values. Above about $10^{17}\ \mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ impurity scattering halves them, so a heavily doped sample conducts far less than $qN_D\mu$ predicts and the resistivity stops falling with doping.

<sub>from ECE-04-01</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using f instead of f_r.** Half-wave ripple frequency is f, full-wave is 2f. Writing 60 Hz into a bridge calculation doubles the answer; forgetting the doubling is the single most common error in this topic.

<sub>from ECE-04-06</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reporting a fill factor above 1.** $FF=V_{mp}I_{mp}/(V_{OC}I_{SC})$ can never exceed 1 because the maximum-power point lies inside the $I_{SC}$ to $V_{OC}$ rectangle. A value above 1 means the wrong $V_{mp}$ or $I_{mp}$ was used, or $V_{OC}$ and $I_{SC}$ were read at a different irradiance.

<sub>from ECE-04-14</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming the unfiltered PIV.** For an unfiltered half-wave rectifier $\mathrm{PIV} = V_m$, but *with* a capacitor-input filter it is $2V_m$ (half-wave and center-tap), because the capacitor holds the peak while the source reverses. Selecting a diode on the unfiltered number burns it out on the first negative peak.

<sub>from ECE-04-06</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating the diode as a fixed 0.7 V battery.** The drop is logarithmic in current and shifts about 60 mV per decade (120 mV when n = 2), so it is 0.55 V at 1 mA and 0.75 V at 100 mA. Any question that changes the current by more than a decade punishes the constant-drop answer.

<sub>from ECE-04-03</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing the sign conventions for $V_P$ and $V_{GS}$.** Entering $V_P=+4\ \mathrm{V}$ in $I_D=I_{DSS}(1-V_{GS}/V_P)^2$ with $V_{GS}=-2\ \mathrm{V}$ gives $(1.5)^2=2.25$ and $I_D=27\ \mathrm{mA}$, four and a half times $I_{DSS}$ and physically impossible. Use the magnitude form $\left(1-|V_{GS}|/V_P\right)^2$ or carry $V_{GS(off)}=-V_P$ with its sign.
