### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $R_D$ alone and ignoring $r_d$.** With $R_D = 3.3\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $r_d = 10\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$ the true AC drain load is $2.48\ \mathrm{k\Omega}$, so quoting $A_v$ from $R_D$ alone overstates the gain by 33 percent.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring $R_L$ in the AC load.** $A_v = -R_C/r_e = -362$ versus the correct $-246$ with $R_C = 4.7\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$ and $R_L = 10\ \mathrm{k}\Omega$: a 47 % overstatement.

<sub>from ECE-05-06</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing $f_T$ with the stage's unity voltage-gain frequency.** $f_T$ is a device figure set only by $g_m$ and $C_{\pi} + C_{\mu}$, measured with the output AC-short-circuited. A real stage's unity-gain frequency also contains $R_L'$ and $R_{th}$, and the Miller zero flattens the roll-off, so the stage's unity-gain frequency is usually below the device $f_T$.

<sub>from ECE-05-13</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Making $g_m$ depend on $\beta$.** $g_m = I_C/V_T$ only. Two transistors at the same $I_C$ with $\beta = 50$ and $\beta = 300$ have identical $g_m$ but $r_\pi$ values that differ by 6 times (1.3 kohm against 7.8 kohm at 1 mA).

<sub>from ECE-05-07</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing peak and peak-to-peak.** $V_{opp} = 3.60\ \mathrm{V}$ peak is $7.21\ \mathrm{V}$ peak-to-peak; quoting 3.60 V as the peak-to-peak swing halves the calculated design margin.

<sub>from ECE-05-02</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Adding the Colpitts capacitors.** The divider capacitors share the same current, so they combine in series: 470 pF with 2.2 nF is 387.3 pF, not 2.67 nF. The sum gives 217.8 kHz instead of the correct 571.9 kHz.

<sub>from ECE-05-17</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reporting a positive $A_v$ for a CE stage.** $A_v = -h_{fe}R_L/h_{ie}$ is inverting: writing 150 instead of -150 flips the phase of every stage in a cascade and reverses the sign of the feedback in an oscillator loop.

<sub>from ECE-05-05</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Putting the CS input resistance at the device instead of the bias network.** $R_{in} = R_G$, typically $1\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$, never $g_m$-dependent. A common failure is to answer $R_{in} = \infty$ and then not notice that the 1 Mohm gate resistor is what actually loads the previous stage.

<sub>from ECE-05-08</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing series and parallel crystal resonance.** At $f_s$ the motional arm is a low resistance (a few tens of ohms) and at $f_p$ the crystal is a high impedance; the crystal behaves INDUCTIVELY only in the narrow band between them - here 1.13 kHz wide, or 0.1% of $f_s$. An oscillator biased exactly at $f_s$ or above $f_p$ fails the loop phase condition and will not run.

<sub>from ECE-05-17</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating GBW as a promise at every gain.** $GBW = |A_v|\times BW$ requires a single dominant pole. At very high closed-loop gain the bandwidth drops below the open-loop dominant pole and the rule no longer describes the circuit, while at low gain the closed-loop bandwidth can approach the second pole, where the extra phase lag causes peaking and ringing even though the magnitude product still looks satisfied.

<sub>from ECE-05-13</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Sign and phase.** CE inverts (180°); CB and CC do not. Writing $A_v = +R_C/r_e$ for a CE stage or $-R_C/r_e$ for a CB stage produces a 180° phase error that the exam asks about explicitly.

<sub>from ECE-05-06</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reversing the topology names.** The order is (input mixing)(output sampling), so series-shunt is the voltage amplifier ($V_o/V_s$, ideal gain $1/\beta$) and shunt-series is the current amplifier ($I_o/I_s$). Writing "shunt-series voltage amplifier" swaps the sample-and-mix roles and produces the wrong resistance table.

<sub>from ECE-05-14</sub>
