### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the buck or boost boundary inductance.** This topology needs $L_{min} = (1-D)^2R/(2f_s)$, and it must be evaluated at the heaviest load: $D = 0.5$, $R = 20\ \Omega$, $f_s = 50\ \mathrm{kHz}$ gives $50\ \mu\mathrm{H}$, ten times that if the load can fall to 200 ohm.

<sub>from ECE-06-06</sub>

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating the dropout voltage as zero.** A 7805 with $V_{do} = 2\ \mathrm{V}$ fed from 6 V delivers about 4 V, and an LDO with 1.5 V of dropout cannot hold 5 V from a 7 V rail that carries 1 V of ripple.

<sub>from ECE-06-09</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Inverting the duty formula to $D = |V_o|/V_{in}$.** For 8 V out of 12 V it gives $D = 0.667$ instead of the correct 0.400, which then corrupts every current and ripple number downstream.

<sub>from ECE-06-06</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Grounding the load to the input return.** The buck-boost output is negative with respect to the input ground, so tying the load return to that ground shorts the converter; use a floating load, or the Cuk or SEPIC which keep the $D/(1-D)$ magnitude with a defined output reference.

<sub>from ECE-06-06</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring $dv/dt$ as a false trigger.** A snubberless thyristor in a circuit with $300\ \mathrm{V}/\mu\mathrm{s}$ edges can be turned on by $C_j\,dv/dt$ through its own junction capacitance even with the gate grounded; the RC snubber is not optional decoration.

<sub>from ECE-06-01</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing the two $m_a$ conventions.** With the whole bus $V_{dc}$ and a load swinging $\pm V_{dc}$ the fundamental peak is $m_aV_{dc}$; with a single leg swinging 0 to $V_{dc}$ it is $m_aV_{dc}/2$. Substituting the wrong one halves or doubles the whole design.

<sub>from ECE-06-08</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Paralleling current-driven devices without ballast.** With $dV_{CE(sat)}/dT=-2\ \mathrm{mV}/^\circ\mathrm{C}$ a die $20\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$ hotter gives away $40\ \mathrm{mV}$ and hogs current — positive feedback that only a $0.1\ \Omega$ emitter resistor (worth $100\ \mathrm{mV}$ per amp) can break.

<sub>from ECE-06-02</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Claiming $V_o=DV_{in}$ at every load.** The ratio is load-independent only in CCM; below $I_{OB}=V_o(1-D)/(2Lf_s)$ the converter enters DCM and the output rises above $DV_{in}$.

<sub>from ECE-06-04</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the average voltage to find the load power.** At $\alpha=60^\circ$ a $50\ \Omega$ load from $120\ \mathrm{V}$ rms has $V_{avg}=40.5\ \mathrm{V}$ and $V_{rms}=107.6\ \mathrm{V}$; the power is $232\ \mathrm{W}$, not $V_{avg}I_{avg}=32.8\ \mathrm{W}$ — a factor of seven.

<sub>from ECE-06-03</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming a capacitor divider can feed an unbalanced load.** Any DC component of load current charges one capacitor and discharges the other, walking the midpoint away from $V_{dc}/2$ and clipping one half of the output waveform.

<sub>from ECE-06-07</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming the SCR turns off when the gate signal stops.** An SCR has no gate turn-off: once anode current exceeds the holding current $I_H$, removing the gate does nothing, and with continuous inductive current the device only stops at natural commutation or when the current is interrupted.

<sub>from ECE-06-03</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Swapping $R_1$ and $R_2$ in the adjustable formula.** $1.25(1 + 240/1500) = 1.45\ \mathrm{V}$ instead of $1.25(1 + 1500/240) = 9.06\ \mathrm{V}$ - a 6x error that no amount of trimming recovers.
