### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing duality with complementation.** Duality swaps AND/OR and 0/1 but never complements a variable; De Morgan complements variables. Applying both at once gives a wrong answer.

<sub>from ECE-08-04</sub>

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing the ASM chart with the datapath.** The chart specifies the controller only. Registers, adders and multiplexers appear in the chart solely as control signals and status flags.

<sub>from ECE-08-15</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming 1's complement has one zero.** It has two: $0000\ldots0$ and $1111\ldots1$ both mean zero, which is exactly why 2's complement replaced it.

<sub>from ECE-08-02</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Breaking a bar without changing the operator.** $\overline{A+B} \neq \bar A + \bar B$ and $\overline{AB} \neq \bar A\bar B$. De Morgan always exchanges AND with OR.

<sub>from ECE-08-04</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Calling $J=K=1$ forbidden.** The JK flip-flop defines that combination as toggle. The forbidden state exists only in the SR latch.

<sub>from ECE-08-10</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing $\bar F$ with $F$ in POS.** $\bar F = \sum m(\text{zero rows})$, while $F = \prod M(\text{zero rows})$. One is a sum of the missing minterms, the other a product of their maxterms.

<sub>from ECE-08-05</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting that a NAND with tied inputs is an inverter.** A NAND-NAND implementation of $AB + CD$ costs 3 gates; drawing an AND before a NAND costs 4 and is not NAND-only.

<sub>from ECE-08-04</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring the complementary outputs.** In a real latch $Q$ and $\bar Q$ are both brought out, and after a forbidden pulse they can both be 0 (NOR) or both 1 (NAND) until the loop settles.

<sub>from ECE-08-10</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing shift directions between transmitter and receiver.** A PISO shifting right emits the LSB first; a SIPO that shifts in the opposite direction reconstructs a bit-reversed word.

<sub>from ECE-08-12</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Sign-magnitude habits.** Complementing only the MSB is sign-magnitude, not 2's complement, and it destroys the addition property. All $n$ bits must be inverted.

<sub>from ECE-08-02</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the same index set for both forms.** $F = \sum m(1,3,5,7)$ is $F = \prod M(0,2,4,6)$, not $\prod M(1,3,5,7)$. The two index sets are complements of each other.

<sub>from ECE-08-05</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reading $C_4$ as the MSB of the sum in a signed system.** For unsigned arithmetic the carry-out is the extra bit; for signed arithmetic it is discarded and the flag of interest is $OV$.
