# Transmission Lines and Waveguides — Trap Recall

### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Scaling by $\varepsilon_r$ instead of $\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}$.** A dielectric filling divides every cutoff by $\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}$. Using $\varepsilon_r$ overshoots the frequency shift by the square root.

<sub>from EST-05-08</sub>

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **One lap is $\lambda/2$, not $\lambda$.** The rotation is $4\pi l/\lambda$, so a full $360^\circ$ corresponds to $l = \lambda/2$ and the printed wavelength scale stops at $0.5$. Dividing a rotation angle by $360^\circ$ instead of $720^\circ$ doubles every distance.

<sub>from EST-05-06</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $\lambda_c = 2a$ for modes other than $\mathrm{TE}_{10}$.** $\lambda_{c,\mathrm{TE}_{20}} = a$ and $\lambda_{c,\mathrm{TE}_{01}} = 2b$. Plugging $2a$ into a higher mode underestimates its cutoff frequency by half and hides the fact that the guide has gone multimode.

<sub>from EST-05-09</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Rounding $f_c$ too early.** Using $f_c = 6.5\ \mathrm{GHz}$ instead of 6.5617 GHz shifts the 8 GHz stretch factor from 0.5721 to 0.5830 and moves $\lambda_g$ from 6.56 cm to 6.43 cm — a 2 % error that is exactly the width of a multiple-choice distractor.

<sub>from EST-05-09</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Applying the $\lambda/4$ inversion at $\lambda/2$.** A half-wave line repeats the load ($Z_{\mathrm{in}} = Z_L$); the inverter rule holds only at odd multiples of $\lambda/4$. For a 100 $\Omega$ load on a 50 $\Omega$ line the two answers differ by a factor of 4.

<sub>from EST-05-05</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $a$ in centimetres with $c$ in m/s.** $f_c = c/(2a)$ needs $a$ in metres. WR-90's $a = 2.286\ \mathrm{cm}$ gives 6.56 GHz in metres but 656 GHz in centimetres — a factor of $10^{2}$ error that looks like a plausible microwave number until you check the band.

<sub>from EST-05-08</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the main-line $Z_0$ in $Z_{\mathrm{in}} = Z_0^{2}/Z_L$.** For the 75/150/300 transformer the correct input impedance is $150^{2}/300 = 75\ \Omega$; substituting the main-line 75 $\Omega$ gives $75^{2}/300 = 18.75\ \Omega$ — a 4:1 error that looks numerically plausible.

<sub>from EST-05-05</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Getting the sign of the required susceptance wrong.** The stub supplies the *negative* of the line's susceptance: with $y_{\mathrm{line}} = 1 + j0.7071$ the stub must give $-j0.7071$, i.e. $\cot\beta l = +0.7071$ and $l = 0.152\lambda$. A sign slip sends the answer to $0.348\lambda$ — a length that looks just as plausible but leaves the load unmatched.

<sub>from EST-05-07</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Return loss is never negative, and it is not the power penalty.** $\mathrm{RL} = -20\log_{10}|\Gamma|$ is positive for any mismatch, and $\mathrm{ML} = -10\log_{10}(1-|\Gamma|^2)$ is a different, much smaller number. At $\mathrm{VSWR} = 2$ they are $9.54$ dB and $0.51$ dB respectively.

<sub>from EST-05-04</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing wavelength and frequency forms of the denominator.** $\sqrt{1-(\lambda_0/\lambda_c)^2}$ and $\sqrt{1-(f_c/f)^2}$ are the same number only if both ratios are formed consistently. Writing $\sqrt{1-(\lambda_0/\lambda_c)}$ (no square) or $\sqrt{1-(f_c/f)}$ gives a wrong stretch factor that still looks numeric.

<sub>from EST-05-09</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Direction confusion.** Toward the generator is clockwise on the standard chart; toward the load is counter-clockwise. Walking the wrong way reflects the point about the real axis and conjugates the impedance — a $+jX$ answer becomes $-jX$.

<sub>from EST-05-06</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **VSWR read from the left-hand crossing.** The VSWR circle meets the real axis twice: the right crossing is $\mathrm{VSWR}$ and the left is $1/\mathrm{VSWR}$. Reading the left one gives a number below $1$, which is impossible for a VSWR and is the giveaway.
