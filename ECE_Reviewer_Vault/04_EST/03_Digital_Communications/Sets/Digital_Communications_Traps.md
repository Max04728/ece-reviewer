### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Believing a CRC corrects errors.** It is a detection code. A nonzero remainder means discard and retransmit — the receiver has no information about *where* the error is, because a CRC is not designed to have the syndrome-as-address property of a Hamming code.

<sub>from EST-03-16</sub>

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing Shannon's capacity with Nyquist's rate formula.** Nyquist's $2B\log_2 M$ is a *noiseless* bandwidth-and-levels limit with no error-probability statement. When both are quoted, the Shannon value binds first, and a Nyquist answer exceeding the Shannon capacity is automatically wrong.

<sub>from EST-03-15</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting PPM needs frame synchronisation.** A PPM receiver cannot distinguish a late pulse in the current slot from an early pulse in the next, so a sync pulse per frame is mandatory. PAM and PWM carry an unambiguous per-pulse reference and do not need it.

<sub>from EST-03-01</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $B = 2f_b$ for QPSK.** The factor 2 multiplies the *symbol* rate, and QPSK's symbol rate is half its bit rate — so QPSK bandwidth is $f_b$, not $2f_b$.

<sub>from EST-03-10</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using a 10-log for a bandwidth ratio expressed in dB.** Bandwidth is a frequency ratio, so the 20-log form applies if decibels are asked for; $2\times$ is $6.02\ \mathrm{dB}$ in that convention, not $3\ \mathrm{dB}$.

<sub>from EST-03-06</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Dividing by $\ln\mu$ instead of $\ln(1+\mu)$.** The normaliser is $\ln(1+\mu) = \ln 256 = 5.545$, not $\ln 255$.

<sub>from EST-03-04</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing self-information with entropy.** $I(x_i) = \log_2(1/p_i)$ is the information in one particular outcome; $H$ is the probability-weighted average. A rare symbol can carry 10 bits while the source entropy is under 1.

<sub>from EST-03-14</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing the null-to-null and Nyquist bandwidth conventions.** $B = 2R_s$ and $B = R_s/2$ differ by a factor of 4 and both appear in textbooks. Check whether the question says null-to-null, minimum Nyquist bandwidth, or quotes a roll-off factor.

<sub>from EST-03-11</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing companding with compression in the source-coding sense.** Companding is a memoryless nonlinearity that reshapes quantization noise; it does not remove redundancy or reduce the bit rate. It changes where the noise lands, not how many bits are sent.

<sub>from EST-03-04</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $2^r \geq m$ instead of $2^r \geq m + r + 1$.** The parity positions themselves can fail and must have their own syndromes, and the error-free case needs one. Dropping the $+r+1$ undercounts parity bits — for $m = 8$ it gives 3 instead of 4.

<sub>from EST-03-16</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing MSK with plain FSK.** MSK is FSK with $h = 0.5$ *and* continuous phase. The continuous phase is what actually tightens the spectrum; plain switched FSK at the same $h$ has much larger spectral sidelobes.

<sub>from EST-03-09</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing up which law belongs to which region.** Mu-law with $\mu = 255$ is North America and Japan; A-law with $A = 87.6$ is Europe and the international standard. Swapping them is the classic regional-standard trap.
