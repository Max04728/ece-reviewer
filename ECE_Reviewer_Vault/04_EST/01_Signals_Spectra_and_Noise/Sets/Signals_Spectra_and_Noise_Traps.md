# Signals Spectra and Noise — Trap Recall

### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring that sampling above Nyquist does not add resolution or remove quantisation error.** It only prevents folding; amplitude resolution and aperture effects are separate limits.

<sub>from EST-01-08</sub>

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring that a loss multiplies downstream noise temperature.** With $G<1$ the term $T_{e2}/G_1$ grows: a 3 dB pad doubles the noise temperature of everything after it, and its own $T_p(1-1/L)$ is added on top.

<sub>from EST-01-06</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing PSD (W/Hz) with ESD (J/Hz).** Random and periodic signals carry spectral *power* density; a single finite-energy pulse carries spectral *energy* density. The two differ by a factor of time, and their units are the giveaway.

<sub>from EST-01-02</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing bandwidths between the signal and noise measurements.** SNR is meaningless unless both powers are measured in the same bandwidth; an audio SNR quoted in a 3 kHz channel cannot be compared with a noise power computed over 1 MHz.

<sub>from EST-01-05</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Expecting linear phase from an IIR filter.** No causal IIR has exactly linear phase; its group delay varies with frequency and a pulse's shape is distorted even when the magnitude response is perfect. If the exam asks for constant group delay, the answer is an FIR with symmetric coefficients.

<sub>from EST-01-12</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting that flat-top sampling still aliases.** The aperture changes replica *shape*, not replica *position*; $f_s \ge 2f_m$ and an analog anti-alias filter are still required.

<sub>from EST-01-09</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using dB gain inside the denominators.** $(F_2-1)/G_1$ needs $G_1 = 100$ for 20 dB. Substituting 20 inflates the second stage's contribution five-fold and can change the answer by several dB.

<sub>from EST-01-07</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating an IIR filter's stability as automatic.** Only FIR filters are unconditionally stable. An IIR is stable if and only if **every** pole satisfies $|z_p|<1$; a coefficient rounding that pushes one pole outside the circle turns a working filter into an oscillator.

<sub>from EST-01-12</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Applying the $-174\ \mathrm{dBm/Hz}$ constant twice.** It already contains the $kT$ factor and the matched-load $\tfrac{1}{4}$. Multiplying by another $V_n^2/(4R)$ double-counts the reference.

<sub>from EST-01-03</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming jitter error scales with amplitude only.** The error is slope times timing error, $2\pi f A\Delta t$, so it is worst at the highest frequency and at the steepest (zero) crossing. Jitter-limited SNR is independent of the number of bits.

<sub>from EST-01-09</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting the +1 when going from $T_e$ to $F$.** $F = 1 + T_e/T_0$; reporting $F = T_e/T_0$ loses the always-present source noise and makes a 60 K receiver look like 0.21 instead of 1.21.

<sub>from EST-01-06</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating $kTB$ as if it depended on resistance.** The available thermal noise power is the same for $1\ \Omega$ and $1\ \mathrm{M\Omega}$ at the same temperature and bandwidth. Only the voltage and current representations change with R.

<sub>from EST-01-03</sub>
