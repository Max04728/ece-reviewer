### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Swapping the ALOHA constants.** Pure ALOHA peaks at $1/(2e) = 18.4\%$ with $S = Ge^{-2G}$; slotted peaks at $1/e = 36.8\%$ with $S = Ge^{-G}$. The slotted scheme is exactly twice as good because the vulnerable period is halved.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Believing 4G dropped voice.** LTE has no circuit-switched domain, but voice is still carried — as VoIP over the packet core (VoLTE). Saying 4G has no voice service confuses the architecture with the service.

<sub>from EST-04-19</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Believing DNS is always UDP.** Queries are UDP/53, but zone transfers and truncated responses use TCP/53. A firewall rule that permits only UDP/53 breaks zone transfers.

<sub>from EST-04-13</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting to escape the escape byte.** A literal `7D` in the payload must become `7D 5D`; otherwise the receiver treats the following byte as a control sequence and the frame is silently corrupted.

<sub>from EST-04-03</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Allocating VLSM subnets smallest-first.** Each block must sit at a multiple of its own size, so a /25 needs a free 128-boundary. If the small blocks are placed first they fragment the space and the large block no longer fits contiguously. Largest requirement first, always.

<sub>from EST-04-10</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing LCP with NCP.** LCP configures the *link* (MRU, authentication, compression, multilink) and runs first. Each NCP configures a *network layer* on top and runs only after LCP is up — IPCP assigns an IP address, for example.

<sub>from EST-04-05</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing up the two window limits.** Go-Back-N is $2^k - 1$, Selective Repeat is $2^{k-1}$. GBN loses one slot, SR loses half the space, and swapping them is the classic error in this topic.

<sub>from EST-04-03</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Calling `fe80::/10` the private range.** Link-local addresses are auto-configured and never routed; the private-address analogue is unique local `fc00::/7`. An `fe80::` address on a router interface is not reachable from another subnet.

<sub>from EST-04-11</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Aggregating by counting networks instead of matching prefix bits.** Four /24s aggregate to a /22 only when they are contiguous and aligned; eight /24s from .16 to .23 aggregate to /21, not /20, because the shared prefix is 5 bits.

<sub>from EST-04-10</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing WDM with TDM or FDM by name.** WDM is frequency-division multiplexing applied to light: each channel gets a wavelength and all channels transmit simultaneously. TDM shares one wavelength by time slots.

<sub>from EST-04-15</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the packet count where the hop count belongs.** With 3 hops there are 2 intermediate switches, and the delay formula uses $N_{hops} - 1 = 2$ extra transmission times, not 3.

<sub>from EST-04-08</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Concluding that cell splitting doubles capacity when the radius halves.** Capacity scales with area, so it quadruples. The practical limit is the increased handoff rate and the cost of the added base stations.
