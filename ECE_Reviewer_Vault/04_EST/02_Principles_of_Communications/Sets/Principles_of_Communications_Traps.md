### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $A_c + A_m$ as the denominator for the index.** $m = A_m/A_c$. The common wrong answer $A_m/(A_c+A_m)$ understates the index by the factor $1/(1+m)$ and turns a 100 percent-modulated signal into 50 percent.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Comparing powers across different load resistances.** $P_c = A_c^2/(2R)$; a problem that changes the antenna resistance between the carrier and modulated cases cannot use the simple ratios. If currents are given, verify that the resistance is the same before applying $I_t = I_c\sqrt{1+m^2/2}$.

<sub>from EST-02-01</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming an FM signal's bandwidth is $2\Delta f$.** That is the wideband approximation; the correct general result is Carson's rule $BW = 2(\Delta f+f_m)$, which reduces to $2f_m$ for narrowband FM.

<sub>from EST-02-05</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using RMS instead of peak message amplitude.** $\Delta f = k_f A_m$ uses the *peak* voltage. Substituting an rms value (for a sine, 0.707 of peak) understates the deviation by 3 dB and is the usual cause of a wrong modulation index.

<sub>from EST-02-05</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Expecting AGC to improve the signal-to-noise ratio.** AGC reduces gain; signal and noise fall together, so the SNR is unchanged. It protects against overload and keeps the audio level constant — that is all.

<sub>from EST-02-14</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Believing a larger $\beta$ is free.** SNR improvement goes roughly as $\beta^2$ but bandwidth goes as $\beta$, and the threshold effect gets worse at large deviation. WBFM's advantage is purchased with spectrum, and the purchase has a floor (the threshold) below which it collapses.

<sub>from EST-02-08</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Linearising through the threshold.** Below about 10 dB input CNR the FM output collapses (clicks) and can be worse than AM's. Any question asking for FM performance at a low input CNR is testing whether you notice that the $\beta^2$ law does not apply.

<sub>from EST-02-10</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting the factor 2 for a sideband pair.** Each order $n\ge1$ has two sidebands, at $f_c-nf_m$ and $f_c+nf_m$, so the pair's power is $2P_cJ_n^2$. Reporting one sideband's power as the pair's halves every higher-order contribution.

<sub>from EST-02-06</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing sensitivity with selectivity.** Sensitivity is the weakest signal that yields an acceptable output SNR (a noise-figure property); selectivity is the ability to reject adjacent channels (an IF-filter property). A receiver can be very sensitive and still useless next to a strong local station.

<sub>from EST-02-14</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting $\rho$ depends on both frequencies.** $\rho = f_{image}/f_{RF}-f_{RF}/f_{image}$; it is not $2f_{IF}/f_{RF}$ and it is not constant across a band. It changes as the receiver is tuned, which is why IRR must be checked at several dial settings.

<sub>from EST-02-13</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Expecting an image-reject mixer to give unlimited rejection.** Hartley and Weaver architectures achieve 40–60 dB, limited by gain and phase mismatch between their two paths; temperature drift and component tolerance set the practical ceiling.

<sub>from EST-02-13</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using simple AGC where delayed AGC is needed.** Simple AGC begins reducing gain as soon as any signal is present, so weak signals are received at reduced gain and the receiver's effective sensitivity is worse than its specification. Delayed AGC holds full gain until a threshold is crossed.
