### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Dropping a pole at the origin.** A factor $z$ or $z^{-1}$ in the numerator changes the power-series coefficients without changing the visible poles; keep it in the fraction until the expansion is done.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating a finite-length (FIR) result as if it needed a stability check for the whole system.** FIR filters are always BIBO stable because $\sum\lvert h\rvert$ is a finite sum; only IIR responses can diverge.

<sub>from MATH-07-05</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting that convolution assumes initial rest.** With stored energy the zero-input response adds to the step response computed here.

<sub>from MATH-07-10</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Calling $t\,u(t)$ bounded.** It grows without limit; boundedness requires a finite bound for all time.

<sub>from MATH-07-01</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using $a^{n+1}$ versus $a^n$ inconsistently in the geometric sum.** The running sum to $n$ contains $n+1$ terms, so the exponent is $n+1$.

<sub>from MATH-07-05</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Saying 'all $z$' for a finite sequence.** Finite sequences exclude $z=0$ if there are positive-index terms and exclude $z=\infty$ if there are negative-index terms.

<sub>from MATH-07-06</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Omitting the ROC.** The ROC is part of the transform, not decoration; without it the inverse is ambiguous.

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Calling a bounded signal an energy signal.** $u(t)$ has $E=\infty$ and $P=1/2$.

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Dividing in the wrong direction.** Long division in $z^{-1}$ gives the causal series; an anti-causal ROC needs division in $z$.

<sub>from MATH-07-08</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Applying the shift before the scale.** For $x(2t-4)$, factor as $x(2(t-2))$: compress by 2, then delay by 2 — not delay by 4.

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reporting the zero-state answer when initial conditions were given.** For a first-order system, $y[-1]$ contributes $a^{n+1}y[-1]$, which can dominate the early samples.

<sub>from MATH-07-09</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing the two versions of $\dfrac{1}{1-az^{-1}}$.** $\lvert z\rvert>\lvert a\rvert$ gives $a^nu[n]$; $\lvert z\rvert<\lvert a\rvert$ gives $-a^nu[-n-1]$.
