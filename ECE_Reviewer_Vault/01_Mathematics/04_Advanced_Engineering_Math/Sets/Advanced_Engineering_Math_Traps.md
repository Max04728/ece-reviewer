### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting that the geometric formula needs a constant ratio.** $\sum 3^{n}/n$ is not geometric; applying $a/(1-r)$ to it produces a meaningless number.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring the radius.** A Taylor series equals the function only inside its interval of convergence. $1/x$ about $x=2$ diverges for $x>4$ however many terms are taken.

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Pairing the columns of $P$ with the wrong diagonal entries of $D$.** The $i$-th column of $P$ must belong to $D_{ii}$. A mismatched pairing still produces an invertible $P$ and a diagonal $D$, but $PDP^{-1}$ is then a different matrix.

<sub>from MATH-04-18</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing the impulse pair with the step pair.** $\delta(t)\leftrightarrow 1$ for all $s$, while $u(t)\leftrightarrow 1/s$ on $\mathrm{Re}\,s>0$, and $\delta(t-a)\leftrightarrow e^{-as}$ is entire. The $e^{-as}$ is a delay factor, not a pole.

<sub>from MATH-04-07</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Off-by-one in the alternating tolerance.** The error after truncating at $N$ is bounded by $b_{N+1}$, so the inequality to solve is $b_{N+1} < \varepsilon$; using $b_N$ gives one term too few.

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting to reduce the power angle.** $n\theta$ can exceed $360^\circ$; an unreduced angle feeds a radian-mode calculator a value that has no relation to the intended direction.

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Writing $A^{k}=PD^{k}$ without $P^{-1}$.** The inverse factor is required on the right for every $k$, including $k=1$; dropping it turns a correct method into a wrong number.

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing up the two value theorems.** The initial value uses $s\to\infty$ with $sF(s)$; the final value uses $s\to 0$ with $sF(s)$. Computing $F(0)$ or $F(\infty)$ instead is dimensionally wrong and usually gives 0 or $\infty$.

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Declaring the endpoints from the ratio test.** At $x = a\pm R$ the ratio equals exactly 1, which is the inconclusive case by construction. The ratio test can never decide an endpoint.

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing the radius with the interval length.** $R$ is the distance from the centre to each endpoint, so the interval has length $2R$ — not $R$.

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Ignoring the one-sided lower limit.** The transform integrates from $0^-$, so it never sees $f(t)$ for $t<0$. Two different two-sided signals with the same $t\geq 0$ behaviour have the same one-sided transform; the convention must be stated.
