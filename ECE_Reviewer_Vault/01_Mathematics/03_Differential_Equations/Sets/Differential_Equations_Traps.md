### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **A repeated root written without the factor $x$.** $(m-m_1)^{2}$ gives $y=(C_1+C_2x)e^{m_1x}$, not $C_1e^{m_1x}+C_2e^{m_1x}$. The second form is one solution wearing two names and cannot meet two independent initial conditions.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting the mode index inside the time factor.** Mode $n$ oscillates at $\omega_n = n\pi c/L$; writing $\cos(\pi ct/L)$ for every term freezes all modes at the fundamental frequency and destroys the waveform's motion.

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reporting the branch without its interval of validity.** $y=1/(1-x^2)$ is a solution only on an interval not containing $x=\pm1$; the board answer expects the largest interval containing $x_0$.

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the Bernoulli substitution when $n=0$ or $n=1$.** With $n=1$ the formula divides by $1-n=0$; with $n=0$ the equation is already linear. Handle those directly.

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Dropping the $v$ in $\frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}$.** This alone accounts for most wrong answers in the topic; without the $v$ the reduced equation does not match the original at all.

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming a nonzero mean is possible.** With $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$ the sine series contains no $n=0$ term, so the average displacement is always zero. A problem giving a nonzero average over fixed ends is inconsistent.

<sub>from MATH-03-12</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Not checking that the ratio is single-variable.** $(M_y-N_x)/N$ must be free of $y$ before it can be integrated as a function of $x$; if it still contains $y$, no such $\mu(x)$ exists and the $\mu(y)$ route must be tried.

<sub>from MATH-03-03</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Not verifying homogeneity before substituting.** If $f(v)-v$ still contains $x$, the substitution is invalid. Check that $M(tx,ty)=t^{n}M$ and $N(tx,ty)=t^{n}N$ for the *same* $n$ first.

<sub>from MATH-03-04</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Writing $\alpha = R/L$.** Dividing $Lq''+Rq'+q/C = 0$ by $L$ gives $q''+(R/L)q'+q/(LC) = 0$, so the coefficient of $q'$ is $2\alpha$ and $\alpha = R/(2L)$.

<sub>from MATH-03-11</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming a linear temperature drop.** Newton's cooling is exponential, so the drop in the second 10 minutes is smaller than in the first. Linear extrapolation overshoots the cooling badly.

<sub>from MATH-03-05</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Feeding $T$ instead of $T-T_m$ into Newton's law.** The governing quantity is the excess over ambient. Using absolute temperature in $T(t)=T_m+(T_0-T_m)e^{-kt}$ removes the $T_m$ term from the initial condition and gives a $k$ that grows with the ambient value.

<sub>from MATH-03-05</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reporting an interior extremum.** The maximum principle forbids an interior hot spot in a source-free steady state; if a computed series peaks in the middle of the rectangle, a sign or a coefficient is wrong.
