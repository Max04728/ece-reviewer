### 1. Evaluate $\displaystyle\int x^3 e^{2x}\,dx$ using the tabular method.

**Given:** cubic times exponential

> [!success]- Answer
> **$e^{2x}\left(\dfrac{x^3}{2} - \dfrac{3x^2}{4} + \dfrac{3x}{4} - \dfrac{3}{8}\right) + C$**

> [!warning] Trap
> Forgetting the successive divisions by 2 in the integral column, which scales every term wrongly.

### 2. Find a reduction formula for $\displaystyle I_n = \int x^n e^{x}\,dx$.

**Given:** derive a recursive relation

> [!success]- Answer
> **$I_n = x^ne^x - n\,I_{n-1}$**

> [!warning] Trap
> Forgetting the factor n from du, which produces an incorrect recursion and wrong values at every subsequent order.

### 3. Evaluate $\displaystyle\int e^{x}\cos x\,dx$.

**Given:** circular case; apply parts twice

> [!success]- Answer
> **$I = \dfrac{e^x(\cos x + \sin x)}{2} + C$**

> [!warning] Trap
> Giving up when the original integral reappears. The reappearance is the mechanism, not a failure. Also forgetting +C after solving for I.

<sub>from MATH-02-04</sub>

### 4. Find the area enclosed by $x = y^2$ and the line $x = y + 2$.

**Given:** better integrated with respect to y

> [!success]- Answer
> **$\dfrac{9}{2}$ square units**

> [!warning] Trap
> Integrating with respect to x, which requires splitting the region into two pieces around the vertex of the parabola. Integrating in y handles it in one integral - this is the payoff for choosing the axis well.

<sub>from MATH-02-09</sub>

### 5. Find the length of the curve $x = \dfrac{y^4}{4} + \dfrac{1}{8y^2}$ from $y = 1$ to $y = 2$.

**Given:** x given as a function of y

> [!success]- Answer
> **$\dfrac{123}{32}$ units**

> [!warning] Trap
> Failing to recognise the perfect square, which leaves an integral that cannot be evaluated in elementary terms. Problems in this form are always built so the radical collapses.

<sub>from MATH-02-13</sub>

### 6. Find the area bounded by $y = x^3$ and $y = x$.

**Given:** curves cross at three points; two separate regions

> [!success]- Answer
> **$\dfrac{1}{2}$ square units**

> [!warning] Trap
> Integrating ∫_{-1}^{1}(x^3 - x)dx in one piece, which gives 0 by odd symmetry. The two lobes have opposite signed area; total area requires splitting at x = 0.

<sub>from MATH-02-09</sub>

### 7. Find the average value of $f(x) = \sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$.

**Given:** trigonometric function

> [!success]- Answer
> **$\dfrac{2}{\pi} \approx 0.637$**

> [!warning] Trap
> Forgetting the 1/pi factor and reporting 2, or making a sign slip in [-cos x]_0^pi: the result is -(-1) - (-1) = 1 + 1 = 2.

<sub>from MATH-02-08</sub>

### 8. Find the value of $c$ guaranteed by the MVT for Integrals for $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0,3]$.

**Given:** average value already found to be 3

> [!success]- Answer
> **$c = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$**

> [!warning] Trap
> Reporting both roots. The theorem's c must lie strictly inside the interval, so -sqrt(3) is discarded.
