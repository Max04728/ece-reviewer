# 03 — ISO 9001 Overview

> [!abstract] Scope
> What an ISO 9001 certificate actually attests, the revision in force, the seven quality management principles, and how a certification body differs from an accreditation body.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> ISO 9001 does not grade the product you shipped; it audits whether the system that produced it says what it does and does what it says — and improves. The certificate belongs to the system, not to the goods.

**What is certified.** ISO 9001 is the certifiable requirements standard for a **quality management system (QMS)** — the *system*, not the product. A certified firm can still ship a defective unit; what the certificate says is that the organization has defined, documented and controlled its processes well enough to meet customer and applicable statutory requirements. The revision in force is **ISO 9001:2015**, which is built on the **process approach** — manage work as linked activities that convert inputs to outputs — and on **PDCA**, which the clause structure follows: Plan (clause 6, planning), Do (clauses 7–8, support and operation), Check (clause 9, performance evaluation), Act (clause 10, improvement). The 2015 edition replaced the 2008 edition, dropped the mandatory quality manual and the separate preventive-action clause in favour of *documented information* (clause 7.5) and *risk-based thinking* (clause 6.1).

**Principles, family and the two bodies.** The **seven quality management principles** are customer focus, leadership, engagement of people, process approach, improvement, evidence-based decision making, and relationship management. The ISO 9000 family is a hierarchy: **ISO 9000** gives fundamentals and vocabulary, **ISO 9001** gives the auditable requirements, and **ISO 9004** gives guidance for sustained success beyond 9001 (it is not a requirement and carries no certificate). Finally, keep the two organizations apart: a **certification body** is the third-party auditor that examines your QMS and issues the certificate, and it must itself be **accredited** by an accreditation body that judges its competence; in the Philippines the accreditation function is handled by the accreditation arm of DTI (PAO).

## Formulas

| Quantity | Expression | Notes |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Standard in force | $\mathrm{ISO}\ 9001:2015$ | Current revision replacing the 2008 edition; a further edition is in development, but the exam answer remains 2015. |
| What is certified | $\mathrm{system} \neq \mathrm{product}$ | Certification covers the QMS; it is neither a product-quality guarantee nor a safety mark. |
| Seven QM principles | $\mathrm{customer\ focus},\ \mathrm{leadership},\ \mathrm{engagement\ of\ people},\ \mathrm{process\ approach},\ \mathrm{improvement},\ \mathrm{evidence\ based\ decisions},\ \mathrm{relationship\ management}$ | Order and count are examinable: customer focus first, relationship management last. |
| Process approach | $\mathrm{input} \rightarrow \mathrm{activity} \rightarrow \mathrm{output}$ | Manage linked processes with defined inputs, outputs, owners, criteria and interfaces instead of isolated departments. |
| PDCA mapping | $\mathrm{Plan}\ (6),\ \mathrm{Do}\ (7\!-\!8),\ \mathrm{Check}\ (9),\ \mathrm{Act}\ (10)$ | Clause numbers follow the cycle; an item that puts clause 9 in the Do phase is testing this mapping. |
| ISO 9000 family | $9000\ \mathrm{fundamentals},\ 9001\ \mathrm{requirements},\ 9004\ \mathrm{sustained\ success}$ | Only 9001 contains auditable requirements; 9000 is vocabulary and 9004 is guidance. |
| Certification body | $\mathrm{third\ party\ QMS\ audit}$ | Audits the company and issues the certificate; must itself be accredited to be recognized. |
| Accreditation body | $\mathrm{accredits\ the\ certification\ body}$ | Judges the competence of certifiers — it audits the auditor, not the factory. |
| Certificate cycle | $3\ \mathrm{years} + \mathrm{annual\ surveillance}$ | Typical cycle: initial certification audit, surveillance audits each year, recertification before expiry. |
| Risk-based thinking | $\mathrm{clause}\ 6.1$ | 2015's headline change: determine risks and opportunities; no separate preventive-action clause remains. |
| Documented information | $\mathrm{clause}\ 7.5$ | Replaces 'documents' and 'records'; a formal quality manual is no longer mandatory. |
| Permitted exclusions | $\mathrm{clause}\ 8\ \mathrm{only}$ | Only operation requirements may be excluded, and only when justified and not affecting conformity. |

## Traps & Exam Notes

- **Saying ISO 9001 certifies product quality.** It certifies that the quality *management system* conforms to requirements; a certified plant can still ship a nonconforming unit.
- **Swapping the certification body and the accreditation body.** The certifier audits your company; the accreditor audits the certifier's competence.
- **Describing the 2008 structure.** ISO 9001:2015 dropped the mandatory quality manual and the separate preventive-action clause, replacing them with documented information and risk-based thinking.
- **Treating ISO 9004 as an auditable standard.** Only ISO 9001 contains requirements; 9004 is guidance for performance improvement and no certificate is issued against it.
- **Excluding clause 9 or 10 as 'not applicable'.** Exclusion is permitted only for clause 8 (operation) requirements, and only when justified.
