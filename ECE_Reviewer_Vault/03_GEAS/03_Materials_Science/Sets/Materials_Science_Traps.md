### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Miscounting atoms per cell.** Using 1 for BCC or 2 for FCC shifts density by a factor of 2 and is invisible unless you sanity-check against a known value. BCC = 2, FCC = 4, SC = 1, HCP = 6.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Believing grain boundaries weaken a metal.** Boundaries are barriers to dislocation motion, so refining grain size *raises* yield strength by Hall-Petch. Removing boundaries (a single crystal) makes the metal softer and easier to deform.

<sub>from GEAS-03-04</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing $kT$ in eV with $E_F$ in joules.** $E_F = 7.0\ \mathrm{eV} = 1.12\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{J}$, and $kT = 4.14\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{J} = 0.0259\ \mathrm{eV}$. Dividing a joule-valued $E_F$ by an eV-valued $kT$ throws the ratio off by $10^{19}$, turning a computed occupancy into either 0 or 1.

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reporting the hysteresis loss in watts per cycle.** The loop area is an energy per unit volume per *cycle* (J/m³); power needs multiplication by frequency and by core volume. A quoted figure of 478 J/m³ becomes 0.24 W at 50 Hz and 95.6 W at 20 kHz in a 10 cm³ core.

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Measuring only one Vickers diagonal.** HV is defined with the mean of the two diagonals. On an anisotropic or slightly tilted specimen the two diagonals can differ by 5%, and since HV goes as $1/d^2$ that is a 10% hardness error.

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Reading a Rockwell dial number directly as a depth.** The dial is inverted and scaled in 0.002 mm increments: HRC = 150 - h/0.002 mm. Larger dial numbers mean shallower indentation and harder material.

<sub>from GEAS-03-06</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Treating $\varepsilon_r$ as a constant.** It varies strongly with frequency (water: 80 at DC, about 2 in the visible), with temperature (ferroelectrics fall sharply above the Curie point) and with field strength in ferroelectric ceramics. A quoted $\varepsilon_r$ without a frequency and temperature is incomplete.

<sub>from GEAS-03-09</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Quoting a Rockwell number without its scale letter.** '48 hardness' is meaningless: 48 on the C scale is a hardened tool steel at roughly 1580 MPa UTS, while 48 on the B scale is soft annealed copper. HRC and HRB use different indenters, different loads and different dial constants.

<sub>from GEAS-03-06</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the instantaneous area to get engineering stress.** Engineering stress uses the *original* area throughout; the falling part of the stress–strain curve after UTS exists only because $A_0$ is held constant while the neck reduces the true area. Using the neck area removes the drop and turns the curve into the true-stress curve.

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Applying $\sigma_{UTS} \approx 3.4\,HB$ to a non-steel.** The constant is empirical for steels up to about HB 400. On a ceramic it is meaningless, because the material does not yield plastically under the indenter — the impression reflects fracture and the 'strength' it predicts can be off by an order of magnitude.

<sub>from GEAS-03-06</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Forgetting that metals have a partly filled band, not a gap.** A 'Fermi level inside a band' is the defining feature of a conductor; a Fermi level inside the *gap* defines a semiconductor or insulator. Reading a mid-gap Fermi level as metallic because 'there are states at $E_F$' inverts the classification.

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming a thicker dielectric is always safer for energy storage.** Doubling $d$ halves the field for the same voltage, so breakdown is less likely — but it also halves the capacitance, so the stored energy $\frac{1}{2}CV^2$ is halved as well. Energy density goes as $E^2$, so the best design runs as close to $E_{bd}$ as the safety margin allows.
