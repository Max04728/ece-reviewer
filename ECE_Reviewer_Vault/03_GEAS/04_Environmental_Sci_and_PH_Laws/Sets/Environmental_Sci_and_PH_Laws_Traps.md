### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Using the generated waste rate for landfill sizing and lifespan. Only the *disposed* fraction after diversion occupies volume, so the remaining life is longer than a raw generated-rate calculation suggests.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Confusing the open-dump timelines: **three years** to convert open dumps to controlled dumps, and controlled dumps banned **five years** after effectivity. Swapping them is the standard error.

<sub>from GEAS-04-06</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Calling the NSWMC a DENR bureau. It is a commission attached to the **Office of the President**, chaired by the DENR Secretary with DENR-EMB as secretariat.

<sub>from GEAS-04-06</sub>

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Treating diversion as incineration with energy recovery. The RA 9003 hierarchy counts re-use, recycling, composting and resource recovery — and separately prohibits incineration of municipal, biomedical and hazardous waste under RA 8749 §20.

<sub>from GEAS-04-06</sub>

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Assuming the food chain with the most links is the most stable. Longer chains lose more energy and are more vulnerable to disruption at any one link; food webs with redundancy are the stable ones.

<sub>from GEAS-04-01</sub>

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Forgetting that the 5-day BOD test is run in the dark. Light would let algae photosynthesise and add oxygen, corrupting the oxygen-consumption measurement.

<sub>from GEAS-04-05</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Quoting the Clean Water Act as RA 8749 or RA 9003. RA 9275 is the Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004; RA 8749 is the Clean Air Act of 1999 and RA 9003 the solid waste law of 2000.

<sub>from GEAS-04-09</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Adding a $\mathrm{CH_4}$ or $\mathrm{N_2O}$ mass to a $\mathrm{CO_2}$ mass without a GWP. Blend the gases only in CO2-equivalent after multiplying by the stated GWP and stating the assessment basis (AR4 vs AR5).

<sub>from GEAS-04-02</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Converting runoff load to concentration with the wrong factor. 1 kg/m³ = 1000 mg/L, so a 100 kg P load in $2.5\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{m^3}$ of runoff gives 0.40 mg/L — twenty times the 0.02 mg/L freshwater eutrophication threshold.

<sub>from GEAS-04-02</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Naming the DENR as the agency for drinking water standards. The **DOH** promulgates and enforces drinking water quality standards under §22(d); the DENR sets ambient water quality and effluent standards.

<sub>from GEAS-04-09</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Calling the import prohibition an 'export' prohibition. Section 13(d) forbids bringing hazardous and nuclear waste **into** the Philippines, even in transit; legitimate export of locally generated waste for treatment is allowed with Basel prior informed consent.

<sub>from GEAS-04-11</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> Quoting a single GWP for methane without the assessment report. AR4 gives 25 and AR5 gives 28; a 12% difference on every methane calculation. State the basis or use the value the question supplies.
