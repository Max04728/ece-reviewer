# 09 — RA 9275 Clean Water Act

> [!abstract] Scope
> Recall the Philippine Clean Water Act by number, year, lead agency and permit system, the water body classes and their beneficial uses, and the exact per-day fines and prohibited acts an examiner tests.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> RA 9275 regulates water by assigning every water body a class first: the class fixes the beneficial use, the use fixes the in-stream guideline values, and any discharge into that body must then meet a general effluent standard keyed to that class — the permit is the document that ties the source to the class.

**The statute and the agencies.** Republic Act No. **9275**, the **Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004**, was approved on **22 March 2004** and took effect fifteen days after publication. It applies to water quality management in **all water bodies**, primarily to abatement and control of pollution from **land-based sources**, while its standards and penal provisions apply irrespective of the source. Section 34 of the Act **repeals PD 984** (the old National Pollution Control Commission decree) and amends or modifies RA 6969, RA 4850, PD 1586, PD 1152, PD 979 and PD 856. The **DENR** is the lead agency and the **Environmental Management Bureau (EMB)** is its implementing arm; the **Pollution Adjudication Board (PAB)** recommends the fines, which the DENR **Secretary** imposes. The special case to memorise: **areas within the jurisdiction of the Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA)** are designated as a single water quality management area **administered by the LLDA** under RA 4850 as amended, and water pollution cases within the Laguna Lake Region follow the LLDA's procedure, though the standards and the wastewater charge system of RA 9275 still apply there. Section 22 shares out the supporting roles: the Philippine Coast Guard enforces standards in marine waters from offshore sources, the DPWH and its attached utilities provide sewerage and sanitation, the DA handles agricultural and aquaculture pollution and re-use of wastewater for irrigation with BFAR responsible for fisheries, the **DOH** promulgates drinking water quality standards, and the DOST evaluates and disseminates cleaner production technology.

**Designation, classification and the permit system.** Section 5 requires the DENR, in coordination with the **NWRB**, to designate **water quality management areas** using physiographic units such as watersheds, river basins or water resources regions; each area is governed by a board chaired by the DENR representative and including mayors, governors, national agencies, NGOs, the water utility sector and business, with a technical secretariat of at least four members. Section 6 requires **non-attainment areas** to be designated where a pollutant already exceeds the guideline value, and new sources of that pollutant may not be added without a corresponding reduction from existing sources. Section 7 directs the DPWH to prepare a **National Sewerage and Septage Management Program** within twelve months. Every point source must obtain a **discharge permit** before discharging regulated pollutants (§27(c) and (i)); operating without it, or after revocation, is a prohibited act. Water bodies are classified under the DENR's water quality guidelines — DAO 2016-08, which superseded DAO 34 (1990) — by best beneficial use: **Class AA** (public water supply requiring only disinfection), **Class A** (public water supply requiring treatment, and primary contact recreation), **Class B** (secondary contact recreation and fishery for propagation and growth), **Class C** (fishery, recreation and industrial supply after treatment), **Class D** (agriculture, irrigation, livestock and industrial cooling), with the marine series **SA** (protected waters, coral reefs and mariculture), **SB** (shellfish and bathing), **SC** (fishery, recreation and shipping) and **SD** (navigation and industrial). The class fixes the in-stream guideline values — Class C, the fishery class, is BOD5 not more than 20 mg/L, DO at least 5 mg/L, pH 6.5-8.5 and total coliform not more than 5,000 MPN/100 mL. Effluent limits are separate: the **General Effluent Standards** of DAO 2016-08 (as amended by DAO 2021-19) set the discharge limits, keyed to the class of the receiving water, with a pH window of 6.0-9.0 and a maximum temperature rise of 3 degrees C.

**Prohibited acts and penalties.** Section 27 lists the prohibited acts, among them discharging or depositing material into or along the margins of surface water where it may be washed in, discharging or allowing seepage into soil or sub-soil that pollutes groundwater, operating a facility that discharges regulated pollutants without a valid permit, unauthorised transport or dumping of sewage sludge or solid waste into sea water, transport or dumping of prohibited chemicals under RA 6969, operating wastewater or sewerage facilities in violation of **PD 1586**, non-compliance of an LGU with the water quality framework and management area action plan, refusal to allow entry, inspection, monitoring or access to records, failure to submit reports or designate pollution control officers, and tampering with or boosting the water supply so as to impair water quality. Section 28 sets the penalties: a fine of **not less than P10,000 nor more than P200,000 for every day of violation**, imposed by the Secretary on the recommendation of the PAB, with the fines increasing by **ten percent every two years** to compensate for inflation, plus the power to order closure, suspension of development or construction, cessation of operations or **disconnection of water supply** until safeguards are in place. Failure to undertake clean-up — willfully or through gross negligence — carries **imprisonment of 2 to 4 years and a fine of P50,000 to P100,000 per day**; if that failure results in serious injury, loss of life or irreversible contamination, the penalty rises to **6 years and 1 day to 12 years and P500,000 per day**. For **gross violation** — deliberate discharge of toxic pollutants in toxic amounts, five or more violations within two years, or blatant disregard of PAB orders such as non-payment of fines or operating despite a closure order — the penalty is **P500,000 to P3,000,000 per day, or 6 to 10 years imprisonment, or both**. Violations of PD 979 (marine pollution from vessels) carry P50,000 to P1,000,000 or 1 to 6 years. Finally, the **Water Quality Management Fund** is created to finance management area programmes, and the Act's clean-up and rehabilitation provisions were the basis of the Supreme Court's continuing mandamus in the Manila Bay case.

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Quoting the Clean Water Act as RA 8749 or RA 9003. RA 9275 is the Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004; RA 8749 is the Clean Air Act of 1999 and RA 9003 the solid waste law of 2000.
- Calling PD 984 the current water law. RA 9275 §34 expressly **repealed** PD 984; the National Pollution Control Commission structure it created is gone.
- Assigning the LLDA to Laguna de Bay cases under the wrong statute. The LLDA administers the Laguna Lake Region under **RA 4850 as amended**, while the standards and wastewater charges of RA 9275 apply there — both statutes are in play.
- Swapping the effluent pH window (6.0-9.0) with the Class C in-stream window (6.5-8.5). The effluent that is legal to discharge can still be the wrong pH for the river, because the two standards answer different questions.
- Saying the PAB imposes the RA 9275 fines. The **PAB recommends and the DENR Secretary imposes** under §28; the PAB's own fining power is the RA 8749 §45 stationary-source penalty.
- Confusing the fine tiers: P10,000-P200,000 per day is the general violation fine; P50,000-P100,000 per day attaches to failure to clean up; P500,000 per day to clean-up failure causing death or irreversible contamination; and P500,000-P3,000,000 per day to gross violation.
- Naming the DENR as the agency for drinking water standards. The **DOH** promulgates and enforces drinking water quality standards under §22(d); the DENR sets ambient water quality and effluent standards.
- Treating the discharge permit and the ECC as the same document. The ECC comes from PD 1586 via DENR-EMB; the discharge permit comes from RA 9275. Operating wastewater facilities in violation of PD 1586 is separately listed as a prohibited act under §27(h).
- Forgetting the two-year escalation. RA 9275 fines rise by 10% every **two** years, whereas RA 8749 fines rise by 10% every **three** years — the statutes differ.
