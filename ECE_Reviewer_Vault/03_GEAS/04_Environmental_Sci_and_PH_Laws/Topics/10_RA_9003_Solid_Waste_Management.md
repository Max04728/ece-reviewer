# 10 — RA 9003 Solid Waste Management

> [!abstract] Scope
> Recall the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act by number, year and administering commission, then the statutory deadlines, diversion and MRF requirements, disposal-facility rules and prohibited acts an examiner tests.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> RA 9003 asserts that waste is a local responsibility and a materials problem: the barangay segregates and recovers, the city or municipality plans for ten years, the national commission sets the framework, and the only thing allowed in the ground at the end is the residue nobody could recover.

**The statute and the institutions.** Republic Act No. **9003**, the **Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000**, was signed on **26 January 2001**. It establishes the policy of a systematic, comprehensive and ecological solid waste management programme and orders the options by preference: source reduction and waste minimisation first, then re-use, recycling, composting and other resource recovery, and disposal only for what remains — 'excluding incineration' is written into the definition of resource recovery itself. Its implementing institution is the **National Solid Waste Management Commission (NSWMC)**, attached to the **Office of the President** and chaired by the **DENR Secretary** with a private-sector vice-chair, composed of **fourteen government members** (DENR, DILG, DOST, DPWH, DOH, DTI, DA, MMDA, the three leagues of local governments, the Association of Barangay Councils, TESDA and the Philippine Information Agency) and **three private-sector members** (NGO, recycling industry, manufacturing or packaging industry) serving three-year terms. The **DENR-EMB provides the secretariat**, headed by an executive director. The **National Ecology Center** provides technical and information support. Primary enforcement and responsibility for solid waste management rest with the **local government units**, which must prepare and submit a **ten-year solid waste management plan** for NSWMC approval. The Act expressly **excludes** hazardous waste, infectious hospital waste and mining waste from the definition of 'solid waste' — those streams belong to RA 6969.

| Transfer station limit | $\mathrm{no\ waste\ stored\ beyond\ } 24\ \mathrm{hours}$ | Section 25. Siting considers the land use plan, proximity to the collection area and haul-route access. |

'Open dump' means indiscriminate disposal with no environmental or health controls. |
| Sanitary landfill | $\mathrm{engineered\ liner,\ leachate\ collection,\ daily\ cover,\ gas\ control,\ monitoring}$ | Section 3(ff). The only permitted final disposal facility. Leachate is the liquid produced when water percolates through decomposing waste; landfill gas is roughly 50-60% methane. |
| ECC before construction | $RA\ 9003\ \S 38:\ ECC\ under\ PD\ 1586\ \mathrm{before\ building\ or\ expanding\ a\ facility}$ | The ECC is a prerequisite in addition to the other permits and clearances; a solid waste facility is not exempt because it is 'environmental'. |
| Open burning prohibited | $RA\ 9003\ \S 48\ \mathrm{bans\ open\ burning\ of\ solid\ waste}$ | Distinct from the RA 8749 §20 ban on incinerating municipal, biomedical and hazardous waste. Two bans, two statutes. |
| Special wastes | $household\ hazardous\ wastes,\ bulky\ wastes,\ consumer\ electronics,\ white\ goods,\ tires,\ batteries$ | Section 3(pp). Collected separately from ordinary municipal waste; toxic components such as dry cell batteries and tires must not be incinerated or landfilled (§28). |
| Non-environmentally acceptable products | $\mathrm{prohibited\ once\ an\ alternative\ exists\ at\ no\ more\ than\ } 10\%\ \mathrm{higher\ cost}$ | Sections 29-30. The Commission prepares the list after public hearing; possessing such packaging in a commercial establishment is a rebuttable presumption of intent to sell. |
| Penalties | $\mathrm{fines\ and/or\ imprisonment\ for\ prohibited\ acts\ under\ } \S\S 48\mathrm{-}49$ | The Act penalises littering, open dumping, open burning and other prohibited acts, and makes an LGU officer administratively liable for failure to comply. Verify the current fine schedule against the statute text before quoting a peso amount. |

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Quoting the solid waste law as RA 9003 of 1999. It is the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of **2000**, signed 26 January 2001; RA 8749 is the Clean Air Act of 1999.
- Swapping the two disposal deadlines: **three years** to convert open dumps to controlled dumps and a ban on controlled dumps **five years** after effectivity. The 3-year figure belongs to conversion, not to closure.
- Placing the MRF at the city level. Section 32 requires an MRF in **every barangay or cluster of barangays**; the ten-year plan is the city or municipal instrument.
- Calling the NSWMC a DENR bureau. It is a commission attached to the **Office of the President**, chaired by the DENR Secretary with the DENR-EMB as its secretariat.
- Sending hazardous, infectious or mining waste to a sanitary landfill. Section 3(kk) excludes all three from 'solid waste'; hazardous waste is governed by RA 6969.
- Confusing the incineration ban with the open-burning ban. Incineration of municipal, biomedical and hazardous waste is banned by RA 8749 §20 (Clean Air Act); open burning of solid waste is banned by RA 9003 §48.
- Treating waste-to-energy as automatically compliant because it 'recovers' energy. The RA 9003 definitions of resource recovery exclude incineration, so a thermal proposal is examined against that exclusion and the Clean Air Act prohibition.
- Assuming a solid waste facility needs no ECC because it is a waste facility. Section 38 requires an ECC under PD 1586 before construction or expansion, on top of other permits.
- Counting only residential waste against the 25% diversion target. Municipal waste includes commercial, institutional and industrial non-hazardous waste and street litter, and the diversion rate is measured against total generation.
