### 1. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Zero speed at the apex.** At the top of a projectile's path $v_y = 0$ but the speed is $v_0\cos\theta$, which is generally not zero. Setting $v = 0$ at the apex makes the horizontal range come out as zero.

### 2. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Measuring angles from the surface instead of the normal.** Every reflection and refraction formula uses the normal; a $60^\circ$ surface angle is a $30^\circ$ incidence angle, and the whole solution shifts.

<sub>from GEAS-02-15</sub>

### 3. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing $g = 9.81$ and $g = 10$ within one problem.** Using 9.81 for the apex height and 10 for the flight time gives answers that disagree by about 2% and do not cross-check. Choose one value at the start and cite it in the answer.

### 4. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Satisfying only $\sum F = 0$ in equilibrium.** A beam with balanced weights can still rotate. Both $\sum F = 0$ and $\sum\tau = 0$ are needed, and the torque condition must hold about *every* axis, so one well-chosen pivot suffices.

### 5. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Using the simple pendulum formula on a rigid body.** A metre stick pivoted at one end has $T = 2\pi\sqrt{2L/3g}$, not $2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$; the point-mass assumption fails and the answer is wrong by about $18\%$.

### 6. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Dropping the $T_s^4$ absorption term.** Net loss is $\varepsilon\sigma A(T^4 - T_s^4)$, not $\varepsilon\sigma AT^4$. At a $13\ \mathrm{K}$ gap near room temperature the emission-only answer is about 15% too large.

<sub>from GEAS-02-10</sub>

### 7. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing rpm and rad/s inside $L = I\omega$.** Angular momentum in $\mathrm{kg\cdot m^2/s}$ requires rad/s. The skater problem happens to work in rev/s because the units cancel, but the moment you compute an energy or a torque the unconverted value is wrong by $\frac{2\pi}{60}$.

<sub>from GEAS-02-06</sub>

### 8. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Dropping $\cos\theta$.** A 60 N force at $25^\circ$ over 8.0 m does 435 J, not 480 J. The perpendicular component of a force does zero work by definition, so overstating by $1/\cos\theta$ is always an overestimate.

<sub>from GEAS-02-03</sub>

### 9. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Assuming a floating body has an apparent weight.** A floating body is in equilibrium, so $F_B = W$ and the apparent weight is zero. Solving for a non-zero apparent weight contradicts the fact that it is not accelerating.

<sub>from GEAS-02-07</sub>

### 10. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Mixing kJ and J in one sum.** $L_f = 334\ \mathrm{kJ/kg}$ while $c\Delta T$ terms come out in joules. Converting only the latent term, or only the sensible terms, shifts the answer by a factor of 1000.

<sub>from GEAS-02-09</sub>

### 11. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Confusing harmonics with overtones.** Harmonics are all integer multiples $n = 1, 2, 3,\dots$; the first overtone is the second harmonic. Off-by-one errors here are the whole item.

<sub>from GEAS-02-13</sub>

### 12. What goes wrong here?

> [!success]- Trap
> **Sign of the corrective power.** A myopic eye needs a diverging lens ($P<0$) and a hyperopic eye a converging one ($P>0$). Treating the near point as a positive image distance flips the sign and prescribes the wrong lens.
