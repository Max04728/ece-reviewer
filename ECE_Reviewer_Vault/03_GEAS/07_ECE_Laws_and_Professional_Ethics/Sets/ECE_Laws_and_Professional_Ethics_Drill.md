### 1. A developer needs the electronics plans and permit application for a building signed and sealed. The design was prepared in-house by a registered ECE. Can the ECE sign and seal it, and if not, who can?

**Given:** preparer: registered ECE; deliverable: electronic plans and permit application; question: who holds sealing authority

> [!success]- Answer
> **No. A registered ECE may prepare the plans but may not sign or seal them; only a PECE may, under Sec. 5(b) and Sec. 29.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Assuming the most senior or most experienced ECE on staff can seal. Sec. 5(b) makes sealing a matter of registrant category, not seniority; a non-PECE ECE who seals commits an act punishable under Sec. 35.

### 2. State the three statutory time limits tested most often under RA 9292: how long the window for ECT registration without examination lasted, when government positions had to be filled by registrants, and when the Act itself took effect.

**Given:** Act approved 17 April 2004; three separate time limits; Secs. 20, 34 and 43

> [!success]- Answer
> **Five (5) years for ECT registration without examination (Sec. 20); three (3) years for government positions (Sec. 34); fifteen (15) days after publication for effectivity (Sec. 43).**

> [!warning] Trap
> Mixing up the two 7-year rules and the several 15-day rules: seven years is the PECE experience requirement (Sec. 18) *and* the ECT registration-without-examination practice requirement (Sec. 20), while fifteen days applies to effectivity, to the release of results and to the appeal to the Commission.

### 3. A corporation applies to the Commission for a certificate to engage in the practice of electronics engineering, offering to employ registered ECEs and PECEs as its technical staff. May it be registered or licensed?

**Given:** applicant: corporation; staff: registered ECEs and PECEs; question: registrability of the entity

> [!success]- Answer
> **No. The corporation cannot be registered or licensed for the practice (Sec. 28); the licence belongs to the individual registrants, who may practise collectively but each answer for their own acts.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Answering 'yes, provided it employs a PECE'. Employment satisfies the sealing requirement of Sec. 29, but Sec. 28 still forbids licensing the entity itself — the registrant, not the company, holds the authority.

### 4. An engineer holds an 'Electronics and Communications Engineer' certificate issued in 2002 under RA 5734. What is his status after RA 9292 took effect, and may he seal plans?

**Given:** valid RA 5734 certificate and ID at effectivity; Act effective 15 days after publication (Sec. 43); question: converted category and sealing authority

> [!success]- Answer
> **He is automatically an Electronics Engineer (ECE) with the same licence number; he may not seal until he qualifies as a PECE under Sec. 18.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Assuming the 2002 registrant becomes a PECE, or that his old 'ECE' title survives. RA 5734 is repealed; the vested right created by Sec. 38 is conversion to Electronics Engineer, not elevation to PECE.

### 5. A foreign PECE is contracted for a one-year telecom project in the Philippines. What must be obtained, for how long, and what local staffing condition applies?

**Given:** foreign PECE, temporarily employed; project duration: 1 year; framework: Sec. 26 exemptions

> [!success]- Answer
> **A Board-issued Special Permit with PRC approval, valid for not more than 6 months and renewable every 6 months, plus at least two Filipino counterparts employed for at least the same duration; no private practice on his own account.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Assuming the permit covers the whole one-year project, or that the foreigner may open his own consultancy. The permit is capped at 6 months renewable, is tied to the specific project, and expressly excludes private practice.

### 6. An ECE board candidate obtains 78%, 74%, 71%, 70% and 65% in the five subjects, passing the majority. What is his status and what happens if he fails the removal examination?

**Given:** ratings: 78, 74, 71, 70 and 65 percent; majority of subjects passed; one subject below 70 but not below 60

> [!success]- Answer
> **He is allowed one removal examination on the failed subject; failing that removal, he is deemed to have failed the entire licensure examination.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Applying a 70% general average. Sec. 16 requires 70% in each subject, and a rating below 60% in any subject removes the removal-examination privilege altogether.

### 7. An electronics technician (ECT) employed by a manufacturing plant prepares and seals the plant's equipment layout drawings. He argues that he has done the work for 12 years and no ECE is available. Is the sealing lawful?

**Given:** registrant: ECT; act: preparing and sealing equipment layout drawings; defence: 12 years of experience, no ECE available

> [!success]- Answer
> **No. An ECT may not seal; sealing is PECE-only under Sec. 5(b), and doing it exposes the technician to the penalties of Sec. 35.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Accepting the 'no ECE available' justification. RA 9292 provides no shortage exception for domestic practice — the only exemptions are the narrow foreign-professional special permits of Sec. 26.

### 8. A PECE is asked to seal a report prepared entirely by another PECE, and separately a colleague whose certificate was suspended last month continues to stamp drawings. Are these violations?

**Given:** case A: sealing a report prepared by another PECE; case B: sealing after suspension; question: exposure under Sec. 35

> [!success]- Answer
> **Both are violations: case A under Sec. 35(f) unless the extent of work performed is clearly indicated, case B under Sec. 35(e).**

> [!warning] Trap
> Believing that a co-signature between two PECEs is routine and lawful. Sec. 35(f) permits it only where the document clearly shows which part of the work each signer actually performed.
