# 04 — RA 7925 Telecom Policy and Carriers

> [!abstract] Scope
> Who may lawfully operate a public telecommunications service in the Philippines, and how a franchise, a CPCN, interconnection, type approval and rate regulation fit together under RA 7925.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> Two gates stand in front of every carrier: Congress grants the franchise (the privilege to be in the business at all), and the NTC grants the CPCN (the authority to actually operate it).

**The statute and its declared policy.** RA 7925, the Public Telecommunications Policy Act of the Philippines, was approved on 1 March 1995 and applies to all public telecommunications entities in the Philippines (§2). Its declaration of national policy (§4) is examinable as a list: develop and maintain a viable, efficient, reliable and universal telecommunications infrastructure; give priority to extending basic service to unserved and underserved areas; treat the radio frequency spectrum as a **scarce public resource** to be administered in the public interest and granted to the best qualified; keep rates and tariffs fair, just and reasonable; let the private sector be the engine of growth; foster a healthy competitive environment; provide fair and reasonable interconnection; rely principally on a stable, transparent and fair administrative process; and — an easy recall item — provide that **no single franchise shall authorise an entity to engage in both telecommunications and broadcasting**, whether by airwaves or by cable (§4(j)).

**Who is who (§3).** A *public telecommunications entity* is any person, firm, partnership or corporation, government or private, engaged in providing telecommunications services to the public for compensation. A *franchise* is a privilege conferred upon a telecommunications entity **by Congress**, authorising it to engage in a certain type of telecommunications service. The recognised categories of entity are the local exchange operator (transmission and switching, primarily voice-to-voice, in a geographic area), the inter-exchange carrier (the carrier's carrier or national backbone operator, connecting local exchanges within the Philippines), the international carrier (transmission and switching between the Philippines and any other point of the world with which it has an existing correspondent or prospective interconnection agreement), and the **value-added service provider (VAS)** — an entity which, relying on the transmission, switching and local distribution facilities of local exchange and inter-exchange operators and overseas carriers, offers enhanced services beyond those ordinarily provided by such carriers.

**The franchise requirement and the CPCN (§16).** No person shall commence or conduct the business of being a public telecommunications entity without first obtaining a franchise. Beyond the franchise, the NTC's authority is the **Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN)**. Two numbers govern its term: the CPCN shall in no case be **shorter than five (5) years**, nor longer than the life of the franchise; and a CPCN expiring at the same time as the franchise is deemed renewed for the same term if the franchise itself is renewed or extended. Two acts do **not** require NTC approval: expansion and financing of networks and services using equipment compatible with or homologous to existing or previously approved plant, to serve additional demand in the same areas; and the upgrading of existing plant and network facilities, including financing, to retire or replace obsolete or outmoded equipment with state-of-the-art equipment within the same areas covered by the previously approved plant. But the Commission may not grant a subsequent CPCN for another segment of service, or extend the area coverage, of an entity that has failed to satisfactorily comply with its commitments to provide a particular service in the original area.

**VAS providers need no franchise (§11).** Provided that it does not put up its own network, a VAS provider **need not secure a franchise**; it may competitively offer its services and expertise and lease or rent telecommunications equipment and facilities for that purpose, in accordance with network compatibility. Where the VAS is offered by a telecommunications entity itself, three additional safeguards apply: prior approval of the Commission so that the VAS offering is not cross-subsidised from the proceeds of the utility operations; no discrimination against other VAS providers in rates and no denial of equitable access to facilities; and separate books of accounts for the VAS.

**Interconnection, rates and spectrum.** Interconnection is defined in §3(k) as the linkage, by wire, radio, satellite or other means, of two or more existing carriers or operators so that the subscribers of one can access or reach the subscribers of the other. Access charge and revenue-sharing arrangements between interconnecting carriers are **negotiated between the parties and submitted to the Commission**; if the parties fail to agree within a reasonable period, the dispute is submitted to the Commission for resolution, which must ensure equity, reciprocity and fairness and take account of the cross-subsidy needed to raise telephone density (§18). On rates (§17), the Commission establishes rates and tariffs that are fair and reasonable, allow economic viability and a fair return, but **shall exempt any specific service from rate or tariff regulation if it has sufficient competition** — while retaining residual power to regulate, and to establish a floor or ceiling, when ruinous competition results or when a monopoly, cartel or combination in restraint of free competition distorts rates and the public is adversely affected. On spectrum (§15), allocation and assignment are subject to periodic review, use is subject to reasonable **spectrum user fees**, and where demand for specific frequencies exceeds availability the Commission shall hold **open tenders** and ensure wider access. Subscribers may use terminal equipment on their premises only if the equipment is **type-approved by the Commission** (§14).

**Who does what between the NTC and the department.** The National Telecommunications Commission is the **principal administrator** of the Act (§5), responsible for entry of qualified service providers, pricing policy for unserved and underserved areas, quality, safety, reliability, security, compatibility and interoperability of facilities in conformity with international standards, mandating fair and reasonable interconnection, protecting consumers against misuse of monopoly or quasi-monopolistic power by investigating complaints and exacting compliance with service standards, and imposing such fees and charges as are necessary to cover the cost of regulation and supervision. The department — the Department of Transportation and Communications when the law was passed, its successors today including the DICT — is responsible for the long-term strategic national development plan, coordination of research and development, representation in international bodies and negotiation of international telecommunications matters, and operation of a national consultative forum (§6). Critically, §6 provides that the department **shall not exercise any power which will tend to influence or effect a review or a modification of the Commission's quasi-judicial functions**.

**Two more recall tables.** Ownership and user rights: all telecommunications entities with regulated types of service must make a bona fide public offering through the stock exchanges of at least **thirty percent (30%)** of their aggregate common stocks within five (5) years from effectivity of the Act or from the entity's first start of commercial operations, whichever is later (§21); and end-users have the right to non-discriminatory, reliable service conforming to the Commission's minimum standards, to be given the first single-line or first party-line telephone connection within **two (2) months** of application against deposit (or within three (3) months after the targeted commencement of service in the barangay, whichever deadline comes later), to regular, timely and accurate billing, and to thorough and prompt investigation of and action on complaints (§20). Equality of treatment (§23): any advantage, favour, privilege, exemption or immunity granted under existing or future franchises ipso facto becomes part of previously granted telecommunications franchises and is accorded immediately and unconditionally to their grantees, except as to territory, life span and type of service. Failure to comply with the local-exchange obligations of an international carrier or a mobile radio operator within **three (3) years** from the grant of authority is a cause to cancel its authority or permit (§§10, 12).

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Answering “the NTC grants franchises.” RA 7925 §3(d) defines a franchise as a privilege conferred by Congress; the NTC grants the CPCN, and §16 makes both a franchise and a CPCN necessary before a public telecommunications entity may operate.
- Saying a VAS provider must always obtain a franchise. Under §11 the franchise is not required **provided it does not put up its own network** — an answer that omits the proviso is incomplete, and a VAS that builds its own network loses the exemption.
- Giving the CPCN term as 25 years or as the life of the franchise only. RA 7925 §16 sets a double bound: never shorter than five (5) years and never longer than the life of the franchise.
- Stating that rates are always regulated. Section 17 requires the Commission to exempt a service with sufficient competition from rate regulation; the residual floor/ceiling power applies only on ruinous competition, monopoly or cartel findings.
- Answering that the DOTC/DICT may revise or review an NTC decision. RA 7925 §6 forbids any departmental power tending to influence or effect a review or modification of the Commission's quasi-judicial functions.
- Confusing the 30% public offering with a foreign-equity limit or a franchise condition. It is a public offering of at least thirty percent (30%) of aggregate common stocks, within five (5) years (§21).
- Treating interconnection charges as NTC-prescribed from the start. The parties negotiate the access charge and revenue-sharing arrangement and submit it; the Commission resolves only a failure to agree (§18).
