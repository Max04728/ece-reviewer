# 02 — Titles: PECE, ECE, ECT and Seal

> [!abstract] Scope
> Which of the three RA 9292 registrant titles a given scope of work requires, what each title takes to earn, and who may sign and seal an electronics plan.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> The three titles are not three ranks of the same job — they are three separate licences with separate scopes, and only one of them (PECE) carries the seal and the exclusive right to consult.

**Three registrant categories, one law.** RA 9292 §4 enumerates exactly three: Professional Electronics Engineer (PECE), Electronics Engineer (ECE) and Electronics Technician (ECT). Each is a person qualified to hold out that title and append that letter set: PECE, ECE, ECT (§3). They are categories of *practice*, not seniority grades — an ECE of twenty years is still an ECE until separately registered as a PECE.

**What separates PECE from ECE.** Under RA 9292 §5 the ECE scope covers the full engineering cycle — investigation, analysis, design, research and development, manufacture, construction and installation, testing, operation, repair, technical support and maintenance of electronic components, devices, products, equipment, systems and networks, including communications, ICT, computers, broadcast, consumer and industrial electronics, electro-optics, avionics, medical electronics and robotics, plus the management, supervision and teaching of those subjects. The PECE scope is *all of the above plus* (a) the sole authority to provide consulting services and (b) the authority to sign and seal electronics plans, drawings, permit applications, specifications, reports and other technical documents. ECT is expressly **non-engineering** work: installation, construction, operation, control, test and measurement, diagnosis, repair and maintenance, manufacture and production, sales and marketing, plus technician-level teaching.

**How each title is earned.** ECE and ECT pass the licensure examination given by the Board under RA 9292 §13, with the scope prescribed in §15 and the ratings rule of §16. PECE does not sit an examination at all. RA 9292 §18 requires instead: a valid ECE certificate of registration and PIC; a valid/current membership identification card from the Accredited Professional Organization; a certified experience record of active self-practice and/or employment in government or private sector spanning **at least seven (7) years inclusive and/or aggregate**, at least two (2) years of which are in **responsible charge of significant engineering work**, counted from the oath date as ECE (or as ECE under RA 5734); and three (3) certifications signed by three (3) PECEs attesting that the record is factual. The Board then schedules an **en banc oral interview** to verify, authenticate and assess the submittals.

**The seal — a PECE instrument only.** RA 9292 §29 requires every licensed PECE to obtain and use a seal of a design prescribed by the Board bearing the registrant's name, registration number and title. Every sheet or page of a plan, drawing, permit application, specification, report or other technical document prepared by the PECE, or executed under his direct supervision and issued by him, must be stamped with that seal, indicating the current Professional Tax Receipt (PTR) number, date and place of payment, and the current APO membership number, whenever the document is filed with government authorities or used professionally. RA 9292 §35 makes stamping after revocation, suspension or removal from the roster a penal offence. Because §32 requires the APO to issue an annual membership card bearing the membership number and validity period, and to require it to be affixed to plans and specifications, the seal block is effectively a four-item block: name, registration number, PTR, APO membership number.

**Validity, CPD and the grandfather edges.** The certificate of registration remains in full force and effect until withdrawn, suspended or revoked (§19); the PIC is the registrant's evidence of registration and, like all PRC PICs, must be renewed. RA 9292 §31 obliges every PECE, ECE and ECT to comply with the continuing professional education/development rules of the Commission and the Board, and RA 10912 §10 makes CPD a **mandatory requirement for renewal of the PIC** for all regulated professions — the electronics CPD Council prescribes the unit count. Two transitory edges are examinable: RA 5734 Electronics and Communications Engineers holding a valid certificate and PIC on effectivity were automatically recognised as ECEs and re-issued a certificate bearing their original licence number (RA 9292 §38); and the Board could register Electronics Technicians **without examination** for five (5) years after effectivity, for applicants with a two-year associate/technician/trade/vocational electronics course (TESDA-certified) or completion of at least the minimum third-year equivalent of the BS ECE/BS Electronics Engineering programme, plus seven (7) years of active practice and three (3) PECE certifications (§20).

| PECE certifications | $\mathrm{3\ certifications\ signed\ by\ 3\ PECEs}$ | RA 9292 §18. They attest only that the experience record is factual; they do not substitute for the Board's oral interview. |
| PECE assessment | $\mathrm{en\ banc\ oral\ interview}$ | RA 9292 §18. No written PECE examination exists — application may be filed with the Commission anytime. |
| PECE membership requirement | $\mathrm{valid\ APO\ membership\ ID}$ | RA 9292 §18. RA 9292 §32 makes every registrant ipso facto an APO member, so failure to maintain good standing makes the applicant delinquent on the roster. |

| ECE examination pass mark | $70\%$ | RA 9292 §16. Must be obtained in each subject; 60–69% in a minority of subjects allows one removal examination only. |
| PECE seal block contents | $\mathrm{name + registration\ number + PTR + APO\ membership\ number}$ | RA 9292 §29. Stamped on every sheet/page of work filed with government or used professionally; omit the PTR and the filing is defective. |
| Signing and sealing authority | $\mathrm{PECE\ only}$ | RA 9292 §5. An ECE or ECT who signs and seals electronics plans is acting outside the scope of his own registration. |
| Consulting services authority | $\mathrm{PECE\ only}$ | RA 9292 §5. Advisory and review, pre-investment or feasibility studies, design, planning, construction, supervision and management services. |
| Unlawful use of title or seal penalty | $\mathrm{P100,000\ to\ P1,000,000\ or\ 6\ months\ to\ 6\ years,\ or\ both}$ | RA 9292 §35. Covers false statements to the Board, using another's certificate/PIC/seal, sealing after revocation, and practising without registration. |
| Certificate of registration validity | $\mathrm{until\ withdrawn,\ suspended\ or\ revoked}$ | RA 9292 §19. The PIC is the separate, renewable identification document. |
| CPD and licence renewal | $\mathrm{mandatory\ for\ PIC\ renewal}$ | RA 10912 §10, implemented for electronics by the CPD Council. Its absence is a renewal problem, not a ground for criminal practice charges. |

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Answering “ECE” when asked which registrant may sign and seal electronics plans and documents: RA 9292 §5 reserves the seal, and consulting services, to the PECE. The correct answer must name the Professional Electronics Engineer.
- Assuming PECE is reached by passing a higher examination. RA 9292 §18 gives no PECE examination at all — qualification is 7 years' aggregate experience (2 of them in responsible charge), 3 PECE certifications, a valid APO membership ID, and an en banc oral interview.
- Reading the PECE experience rule as “7 years, 2 of which are anything.” The 2 years must be in responsible charge of significant engineering work, and the 7 years run inclusive/aggregate from the date the applicant took the oath as ECE — not from graduation or from first employment.
- Treating the 3 PECE certifications as the qualification itself. They vouch only that the submitted experience record is factual; the Board still conducts the en banc oral interview to establish competency.
- Calling ECT an “assistant engineer” or a junior ECE. RA 9292 §5 defines the ECT scope as any non-engineering work or activity — answering “ECT may design, provided a PECE seals it” is wrong; design is outside the ECT scope altogether.
- Quoting CPE (continuing professional education, the RA 9292 §31 wording) when the exam asks what law governs renewal today: RA 10912 is the Continuing Professional Development Act of 2016 and it is CPD that RA 10912 §10 makes mandatory for PIC renewal.
- Presenting the ECT registration-without-examination window as an available route. RA 9292 §20 allowed it only within five (5) years after effectivity (2004), for 7 years of practice plus three PECE certifications; the window has closed.
