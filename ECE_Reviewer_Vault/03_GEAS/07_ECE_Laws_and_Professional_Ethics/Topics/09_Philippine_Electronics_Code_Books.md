# 09 — Philippine Electronics Code Books

> [!abstract] Scope
> What legal force the Philippine Electronics Code has, which authority issues it, and how it is distinguished from the Philippine Electrical Code and the National Building Code.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> Three documents, three issuers: the building code sets where and how structures go up, the electrical code wires them, and the electronics code governs the electronic and communications systems installed in them.

**Where the Philippine Electronics Code comes from.** RA 9292 does not itself annex a code. It gives the Professional Regulatory Board of Electronics Engineering the rule-making authority that produces one, in two places. First, RA 9292 §7 empowers the Board to “promulgate rules and regulations on the scope of practice” of PECEs, ECEs and ECTs, and to “formulate, prescribe and adopt such rules and regulations for **electronics installations** in industrial plants, commercial establishments and other buildings or structures covered by the National Building Code of the Philippines, in coordination with the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), other concerned government agencies, representatives of industry and the Accredited Professional Organization”. Second, RA 9292 §39 requires the Board, subject to the approval of the Commission and in coordination with the accredited professional organization, to adopt and promulgate such rules, regulations and resolutions — together with the Code of Ethics and the Code of Technical Standards of Practice — to carry out the Act, published in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation and effective fifteen (15) days after publication. The **Philippine Electronics Code** is the technical document issued under that authority: a multi-volume set of installation and practice standards for electronic, communications and related systems, forming part of the rules and regulations implementing RA 9292. It is periodically revised by the Board — the familiar form of a revision is a PRC/Board resolution promulgating the new edition, published and then effective after the statutory publication period. Because revisions are republished, the examinable points are the **issuer** (the Professional Regulatory Board of Electronics Engineering, with Commission approval and DPWH coordination for building-related installations) and the **legal character** (part of the rules and regulations under RA 9292 §§7 and 39, not a separate statute).

**What the volumes cover.** The set is organised by subject area, and the board-exam recall list is the coverage rather than a remembered volume number: a volume on **radio spectrum** and radio communication systems; a volume on **radio and television broadcast** systems; a volume on **telecommunications** plant, outside plant and related facilities; and a volume on **electrical and electronic safety** — protection of persons and property, wiring and installation requirements for electronic and communications equipment. Related practice material (installation standards, safety requirements, and the technical standards of practice for the profession) belongs to the Board's Code of Technical Standards of Practice under §30. If a question names a specific volume number or edition year, answer from the citation given in the question: the number of volumes and their titles have changed across editions, so a memorised volume number is a liability rather than an asset. What is safe to assert is the subject grouping — spectrum, broadcast, telecommunications, and electrical/electronic safety and installation — and that every volume is issued by the same Board under the same statutory authority.

**How it differs from the Philippine Electrical Code.** The **Philippine Electrical Code (PEC)** is issued under a different profession's regulation — it is promulgated by the **Professional Regulatory Board of Electrical Engineering** (with the Commission's approval and, historically, in cooperation with the Institute of Integrated Electrical Engineers of the Philippines) as the implementing technical standard for the practice of electrical engineering and for electrical installations. The **Philippine Electronics Code** is issued by the **Board of Electronics Engineering** under RA 9292. The two overlap at the boundary, and that boundary is the exam point: power distribution, service entrances, branch circuits, grounding of electrical systems, motors and lighting are electrical-code territory, while electronic and communications systems — radio and broadcast facilities, telecommunications and data cabling systems, electronic safety and signalling systems, and the associated equipment installation — are electronics-code territory. Because both codes derive their force from the rules and regulations of their own professional regulatory law and are adopted by reference in building permitting, an installation can be required to comply with both, and a document prepared for one profession's scope cannot be sealed by the other: only a PECE may sign and seal electronics plans under RA 9292 §5, and the electrical counterpart is reserved to the registered electrical engineer's own seal authority.

**How it relates to the National Building Code and to the permit chain.** The National Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096) governs the design, location, construction, use and maintenance of buildings and structures, and it is the statute that **incorporates the referral codes** — the structural, electrical, mechanical, sanitary and electronics standards — as part of its own implementing rules. RA 9292 §7 assumes that relationship expressly when it directs the Board to formulate rules for electronics installations in structures covered by the National Building Code, in coordination with the DPWH and the other agencies and industry representatives. The practical consequence is the permit chain: for a building or commercial establishment, the electronics plans, drawings, permit applications, specifications and reports are prepared, signed and sealed by a **PECE** (RA 9292 §5 and §29), stamped on every sheet with the seal bearing his name, registration number and title, and indicating his current Professional Tax Receipt number, date and place of payment, and current APO membership number, before they are filed with the government authority. Because RA 9292 §7 also authorises the Board to prescribe **minimum manning and manpower requirements** for PECEs, ECEs and ECTs in industrial plants and commercial establishments, and to conduct ocular inspection of those premises — personally or through authorised Commission employees or authorised APO members, with Commission approval — the electronics code is not a purely advisory document: it is the standard against which compliance in the plant or establishment is measured.

**Recall frame for the three documents.** Keep four columns straight — document, issuing authority, statutory anchor, and subject matter. The *Philippine Electronics Code*: Board of Electronics Engineering, RA 9292 §§7 and 39 (with DPWH coordination under §7 for buildings covered by the National Building Code), covering electronic, communications, broadcast, spectrum and electronic-safety installations. The *Philippine Electrical Code*: Board of Electrical Engineering, under the electrical engineering practice law and the same Commission-approval mechanism, covering electrical wiring and power installations. The *National Building Code*: PD 1096, implemented by the DPWH through its IRR, covering building and structural requirements and adopting the referral codes. A question that asks “who issues the Philippine Electronics Code?” wants the **Board of Electronics Engineering**; a question that asks which law makes it enforceable wants **RA 9292** (with PD 1096 for the referral mechanism in buildings); and a question that asks who may sign and seal the resulting electronics documents wants the **PECE**.

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Answering “the IIEE” or “the Board of Electrical Engineering” for who issues the Philippine Electronics Code. The electronics code is issued by the Professional Regulatory Board of Electronics Engineering under RA 9292 §§7 and 39; the electrical board issues the Philippine Electrical Code.
- Treating the Philippine Electronics Code as a statute. It is a technical code issued as part of the rules and regulations implementing RA 9292, published in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation and effective fifteen (15) days after publication (RA 9292 §39).
- Confusing PEC (Philippine Electrical Code) with PEC (Philippine Electronics Code) as an abbreviation. In a question, spell out which code is meant; the two have different issuers, different scopes and different sign-and-seal authorities.
- Saying an ECE may sign and seal electronics plans provided a PECE reviews them. RA 9292 §5 reserves signing and sealing to the PECE and makes sealing without authority, or sealing another PECE's work without indicating the part actually performed, a penal offence under §35.
- Quoting a specific volume number or edition year for the electronics code from memory. Successive revisions have changed the set and its titles; state the subject coverage (spectrum, broadcast, telecommunications, electrical/electronic safety) unless the question supplies the citation.
- Omitting the DPWH coordination when the installation is in a building covered by the National Building Code. RA 9292 §7 requires the Board to formulate those rules in coordination with the DPWH and other concerned agencies, industry representatives and the APO.
- Forgetting that the code's enforcement reaches into establishments, not only construction documents. RA 9292 §7 lets the Board prescribe minimum manning and manpower requirements in industrial plants and commercial establishments and authorises ocular inspection for compliance.
