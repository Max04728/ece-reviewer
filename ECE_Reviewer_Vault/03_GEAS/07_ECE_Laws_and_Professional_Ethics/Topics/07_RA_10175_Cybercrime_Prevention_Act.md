# 07 — RA 10175 Cybercrime Prevention Act

> [!abstract] Scope
> Which acts RA 10175 punishes, what each category costs in penalty, and which agency investigates, prosecutes, coordinates or preserves the evidence.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> The Cybercrime Prevention Act divides offences by what they attack — the machine (confidentiality, integrity, availability), the transaction (computer-related), or the content (content-related) — and the category decides the penalty.

**The statute and the shape of Section 4.** RA 10175, the Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012, was approved on 12 September 2012. Section 4 defines cybercrime offences in three groups. **(a) Offences against the confidentiality, integrity and availability of computer data and systems**: illegal access (access to the whole or any part of a computer system **without right**); illegal interception (interception by technical means, without right, of any non-public transmission of computer data to, from or within a computer system, including electromagnetic emissions from a computer system carrying such data); data interference (the intentional or reckless alteration, damaging, deletion or deterioration of computer data, an electronic document or an electronic data message, without right, **including the introduction or transmission of viruses**); system interference (the intentional alteration or reckless hindering or interference with the functioning of a computer or computer network by inputting, transmitting, damaging, deleting, deteriorating, altering or suppressing computer data, a program, an electronic document or an electronic data message, without right or authority, likewise including viruses); **misuse of devices** (the use, production, sale, procurement, importation, distribution or otherwise making available, without right, of a device including a computer program designed or adapted primarily for committing any offence under the Act, or of a computer password, access code or similar data by which the whole or any part of a computer system is capable of being accessed, with intent that it be used to commit an offence, and the possession of such an item with that intent); and **cyber-squatting** (acquisition of a domain name in bad faith to profit, mislead, destroy reputation, and deprive others of registering the same, where the name is similar, identical or confusingly similar to an existing registered trademark, or identical or similar to the name of a person other than the registrant, and is acquired without right or with intellectual property interests in it).

**Section 4(b) and 4(c).** **(b) Computer-related offences**: computer-related forgery (input, alteration or deletion of computer data without right resulting in inauthentic data with intent that it be considered or acted upon for legal purposes as if authentic, or knowingly using such data for a fraudulent or dishonest design); computer-related fraud (unauthorised input, alteration or deletion of computer data or program, or interference in the functioning of a computer system, causing damage with fraudulent intent); and computer-related identity theft (intentional acquisition, use, misuse, transfer, possession, alteration or deletion of identifying information belonging to another, natural or juridical, without right). For both fraud and identity theft, **if no damage has yet been caused the penalty imposable is one (1) degree lower**. **(c) Content-related offences**: cybersex (the wilful engagement, maintenance, control or operation, directly or indirectly, of any lascivious exhibition of sexual organs or sexual activity, with the aid of a computer system, for favour or consideration); child pornography (the unlawful or prohibited acts defined and punishable by RA 9775, the Anti-Child Pornography Act of 2009, committed through a computer system, with the penalty **one (1) degree higher** than that provided in RA 9775); unsolicited commercial communications (transmission of commercial electronic communication by computer system to advertise, sell or offer for sale products and services, prohibited unless there is prior affirmative consent from the recipient, or the primary intent is service and/or administrative announcements from the sender to its existing users, subscribers or customers, or the message contains a simple, valid and reliable opt-out and does not disguise its source or include misleading information); and libel (the unlawful or prohibited acts of libel under Article 355 of the Revised Penal Code, as amended, committed through a computer system or any other similar means that may be devised in the future).

**Other offences and the escalation rules.** Section 5 makes **aiding or abetting** and **attempt** separate offences. Section 6 provides that all crimes already defined and penalised by the Revised Penal Code, as amended, and by special laws, if committed by, through and with the use of information and communications technologies, are covered by the Act, and the penalty to be imposed is **one (1) degree higher** than that provided by the Revised Penal Code or the special law. Section 7 preserves liability under other laws — prosecution under RA 10175 is without prejudice to liability under the Revised Penal Code or special laws. Note the direction of travel: one degree **higher** for RPC and special-law crimes committed through ICT and for child pornography; one degree **lower** only for aiding/abetting and attempt under Section 5, and for computer-related fraud and identity theft where no damage has yet been caused. A related rule from the practitioner's side: the penalty for **online libel** is likewise raised, and Section 6's one-degree-higher rule is the mechanism.

**Who enforces and who coordinates.** Section 10 places law-enforcement responsibility on the **National Bureau of Investigation (NBI)** and the **Philippine National Police (PNP)**, each of which must organise a cybercrime unit or centre manned by special investigators to handle violations of the Act exclusively. Section 11 requires those computer or technology crime divisions to submit timely and regular reports — pre-operation, post-operation and investigation results — to the **Department of Justice** for review and monitoring, and Section 23 creates an **Office of Cybercrime within the DOJ** as the central authority in all matters related to international mutual assistance and extradition. Section 24 creates the **Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC)**, an inter-agency body under the administrative supervision of the Office of the President, to coordinate policy and formulate and enforce the national cybersecurity plan. The CICC is chaired by the Executive Director of the Information and Communications Technology Office under the Department of Science and Technology (ICTO-DOST), with the **Director of the NBI as Vice Chairperson**, and the Chief of the PNP, the head of the DOJ Office of Cybercrime, and one representative from the private sector and academe as members (Section 25). On the procedure: law-enforcement authorities may collect or record traffic data in real time with due cause, but all other data to be collected, seized or disclosed requires a court warrant (Section 12); service providers must preserve traffic data and subscriber information for a minimum of six (6) months from the date of the transaction, and content data for six (6) months from receipt of the order, with a one-time extension of another six (6) months (Section 13); and upon securing a court warrant, law-enforcement authorities may order any person or service provider to disclose or submit subscriber information, traffic data or relevant data **within seventy-two (72) hours** from receipt of the order (Section 14). Evidence procured without a valid warrant or beyond its authority is inadmissible (Section 18); the DOJ may order the restriction or blocking of access to computer data prima facie in violation of the Act (Section 19); and failure to comply with the orders of law-enforcement authorities under Chapter IV is punished as a violation of PD 1829 with imprisonment of prision correccional in its maximum period or a fine of ₱100,000, or both, for each and every noncompliance (Section 20).

**The penalty table and corporate liability.** Section 8: any person guilty of an act under Section 4(a) or 4(b) is punished with imprisonment of **prision mayor** or a fine of at least ₱200,000 up to a maximum amount commensurate to the damage incurred, or both; an act under Section 4(a)(5) (misuse of devices) is punished with prision mayor or a fine of **not more than ₱500,000**, or both; and if the acts in Section 4(a) are committed against **critical infrastructure**, the penalty is **reclusion temporal** or a fine of at least ₱500,000 up to a maximum amount commensurate to the damage incurred, or both. Acts under Section 4(c)(1) (cybersex) carry prision mayor or a fine of at least ₱200,000 but not exceeding ₱,000,000, or both; acts under Section 4(c)(2) (child pornography through a computer system) carry the penalties in RA 9775 but **one (1) degree higher**; acts under Section 4(c)(3) (unsolicited commercial communications) carry imprisonment of **arresto mayor** or a fine of at least ₱50,000 but not exceeding ₱250,000, or both; and acts under Section 5 (aiding, abetting, attempt) carry imprisonment **one (1) degree lower** than the prescribed penalty for the offence, or a fine of at least ₱100,000 but not exceeding ₱500,000, or both. Section 9 adds corporate liability: where an offence is knowingly committed on behalf of or for the benefit of a juridical person by a natural person with a leading position (power of representation, authority to take decisions, or authority to exercise control), the juridical person is liable for a fine of at least double the fines imposable up to a maximum of **₱10,000,000**; where the offence was made possible by lack of supervision or control, the fine is at least double up to a maximum of **₱5,000,000**. Jurisdiction lies with the **Regional Trial Court**, including over a violation committed by a Filipino national regardless of the place of commission, and special cybercrime courts manned by specially trained judges are to be designated (Section 21).

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Answering that the DOJ investigates cybercrime. RA 10175 §10 gives law-enforcement responsibility to the **NBI and the PNP**; the DOJ reviews and monitors the reports (§11) and hosts the Office of Cybercrime as the international-assistance central authority (§23).
- Saying the CICC is chaired by the DOJ or the DICT Secretary. Section 25 chairs it with the **Executive Director of the ICTO-DOST**, with the NBI Director as Vice Chairperson.
- Applying the one-degree-higher rule to aiding, abetting or attempt. Section 5 penalties are **one degree lower** than the prescribed penalty for the offence, or a fine of ₱100,000 to ₱500,000, or both; the one-degree-higher rule belongs to §6 (RPC and special-law crimes through ICT) and §4(c)(2) (child pornography).
- Quoting the critical-infrastructure penalty as prision mayor. Section 8 raises Section 4(a) acts against critical infrastructure to **reclusion temporal** with a fine of at least ₱500,000; prision mayor with a fine of at least ₱200,000 is the base row for 4(a) and 4(b).
- Mixing up the 72 hours and the 6 months. The six (6)-month period in §13 is the data **preservation** duty of service providers; the seventy-two (72) hours in §14 is the deadline to **disclose** subscriber information, traffic data or relevant data after a court warrant.
- Answering that the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center prosecutes cases. Section 26 gives it policy coordination — a national cybersecurity plan, a computer emergency response team, monitoring, international cooperation and recommendations — not prosecutorial power.
- Treating misuse of devices as punishable only when the device is used. Section 4(a)(5) also covers production, sale, procurement, importation, distribution or making available, and mere **possession with intent** to use the item to commit an offence.
- Forgetting that computer-related fraud and identity theft soften when no damage is caused. Both carry a penalty one (1) degree lower if no damage has yet been caused — an easy omission because the rest of the Act only escalates.
