# 05 — NTC Mandates and Regulations

> [!abstract] Scope
> What the National Telecommunications Commission is, where its powers come from, and what it may do to a station, an operator or a piece of radio equipment.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> RA 7925 tells the NTC what to regulate; EO 546 is where the NTC itself, and most of its licensing machinery over the airwaves, actually comes from.

**Origin and placement.** The National Telecommunications Commission was established on 23 July 1979 by Executive Order No. 546, which integrated two abolished bodies into one agency: the **Board of Communications** (the first quasi-judicial body with adjudicatory powers over telecommunications services, created under the Integrated Reorganization Plan, PD 1) and the **Telecommunications Control Bureau** (the former Radio Control Office, which itself traces to the Radio Control Law, Act No. 3846 of 1931 and earlier to the Ship Radio Station Law, Act No. 3396 of 1927). EO 546 placed the Commission under the Ministry of Transportation and Communications, with the express proviso that in respect of its quasi-judicial functions its decisions are appealable in the same manner as decisions of the Board of Communications had been appealed (§16). Its attachment has since moved: EO 125-A (1987) made it an attached agency of the DOTC; EO 269 (2004) moved it to the CICT; EO 454 (2005) returned it to the DOTC; EO 648 (2008) moved it to the CICT; EO 47 (2011) retained it under the Office of the President as part of the Other Executive Offices; and RA 10844 (2016), which created the Department of Information and Communications Technology, made the NTC an attached agency of the DICT. Today's exam answer for “under which department?” is **the DICT**.

**What EO 546 §15 empowers the Commission to do.** Issue a Certificate of Public Convenience for the operation of communications utilities and services, radio communications systems, wire or wireless telephone or telegraph systems, radio and television broadcasting systems and other similar public utilities; establish, prescribe and regulate the areas of operation of particular operators of public service communications and determine and prescribe charges or rates (except where the rate is fixed by an international body of which the Philippines is a member, or by a body recognised by the Philippine Government as the proper arbiter); **grant permits for the use of radio frequencies** for wireless telephone and telegraph systems and radio communication systems, including amateur radio stations, and for radio and television broadcasting systems; **sub-allocate series of frequencies of bands allocated by the International Telecommunication Union** to specific services; establish and prescribe rules, regulations, standards and specifications in all cases related to the issued Certificate of Public Convenience and administer and enforce the same; coordinate with other agencies concerned with communications; promulgate rules to encourage a larger and more effective use of communications, radio and television broadcasting facilities and to maintain effective competition whenever reasonably feasible; **supervise and inspect the operation of radio stations and telecommunications facilities**; **undertake the examination and licensing of radio operators**; and undertake, whenever necessary, **the registration of radio transmitters and transceivers**. §17 exempts telecommunications stations owned and operated by the Armed Forces of the Philippines from the Executive Order, except that they must use frequencies as allowed or assigned by the Commission.

**Structure and the quasi-judicial side.** EO 546 §16: the Commission is composed of a Commissioner and two (2) Deputy Commissioners, preferably one a lawyer and another an economist, all of unqualified integrity, proven competence and recognised expertise in fields related as much as possible to communications; it is under the supervision and control of the ministry except that its quasi-judicial decisions remain appealable as before. Its adjudicatory toolkit comes from the Public Service Law it inherited: it sits on notice and hearing, may issue certificates of public convenience, fix individual or joint rates, tolls and charges, fix just and reasonable standards, classifications, regulations, practices, measurement and service, require safe, adequate and proper service and reasonable extensions of facilities, and **amend, modify or revoke a certificate at any time whenever the facts and circumstances on the strength of which it was issued have been misrepresented or materially changed** (CA 146 §16, as modified by PD 1 and EO 546). It may issue subpoenas and subpoenas duces tecum in any matter pending before it, and disobedience may be taken to the courts (CA 146 §30).

**Statutory definitions that anchor the licensing power.** CA 146 §13(a) gives the Commission jurisdiction, supervision and control over all public services and their franchises, equipment and other properties, and §13(b) includes within “public service” any wire or wireless communications system and any wire or wireless broadcasting station. Section 15 forbids any public service to operate in the Philippines without a valid and subsisting “certificate of public convenience” or “certificate of public convenience and necessity”, and §16(a) limits such certificates to citizens of the Philippines and to entities organised under Philippine law with at least sixty per centum of the stock or paid-up capital owned by Philippine citizens, for a period of not more than fifty (50) years. RA 7925 then layers the sector-specific rules on top: the NTC is the **principal administrator** of that Act (RA 7925 §5), the radio frequency spectrum is a scarce public resource whose use is subject to reasonable **spectrum user fees**, with **open tenders** where demand exceeds availability (§15), and equipment may be used by subscribers only when **type-approved by the Commission** (§14).

**What is prescribed by NTC rules rather than by the statutes.** Several items in the exam's memory list come from the Commission's own issuances under EO 546 §15(e) and RA 7925 §5, not from the two laws: the specific classes of **radio operator's certificate** and their examination, the **Radio Station Licence (RSL)** and construction/operation permits for stations, permits to purchase, possess, sell or transfer radio transceivers, the schedules of **spectrum user fees**, the **type approval** (of a model/type) and **type acceptance** (of a specific unit) of radio and telecommunications equipment, and the administrative penalties and the recall or confiscation of unregistered or non-compliant devices that follow. Two things are safely examinable: that the power to impose these exists in EO 546 §15 (licensing of operators, registration of transmitters and transceivers, prescription of standards) and RA 7925 §§5, 14 and 15 (fees to cover the cost of regulation, type approval, spectrum user fees); and that the exact fee amounts and penalty schedules change by memorandum circular, so a board question will state them if it needs them.

**Appeal and review.** Because EO 546 §16 preserved the old appeal route for its quasi-judicial functions, an NTC decision is not reviewed by the DICT — the same rule appears in RA 7925 §6, which forbids the department from exercising any power tending to influence or effect a review or modification of the Commission's quasi-judicial functions. The Public Service Law route ran to the Supreme Court on certiorari or petition for review, filed within thirty (30) days from notice of the order, ruling or decision, or within fifteen (15) days after notice of an order denying reconsideration (CA 146 §36).

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Saying the DICT regulates telecommunications day to day. The NTC is the regulator and the principal administrator of RA 7925; the DICT sets policy and is expressly barred by RA 7925 §6 from reviewing or modifying the NTC's quasi-judicial functions.
- Crediting the NTC's creation to RA 7925. The Commission was created by EO 546 in 1979; RA 7925 (1995) added responsibilities to an existing agency and named it principal administrator.
- Treating a congressional franchise and an NTC certificate as alternatives. CA 146 §15 requires a certificate of public convenience or CPCN to operate, and RA 7925 §16 separately requires a franchise, with the CPCN never shorter than five years and never longer than the life of the franchise.
- Answering that the NTC allocates frequency bands. EO 546 §15(d) empowers it only to **sub-allocate** series of frequencies within bands allocated by the International Telecommunication Union.
- Quoting exact spectrum user fee rates, type approval charges or recall penalties from memory. Those are set and revised by NTC memorandum circular, so a question that needs a number will supply it; state the power and the instrument instead of inventing a figure.
- Stating that the Armed Forces of the Philippines is fully exempt from NTC authority. EO 546 §17 exempts AFP stations from the Order but still requires them to use frequencies allowed or assigned by the Commission.
- Answering that a 1936 Public Service Law citizenship clause still allows United States citizens parity in a public service. CA 146 §16(a) as originally written did; the 1987 Constitution's 60% Filipino requirement governs today.
