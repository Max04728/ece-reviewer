# 08 — DOLE DO 13 OSHS Safety

> [!abstract] Scope
> What the Occupational Safety and Health Standards require of an employer, what a safety officer and a safety and health committee must exist in a given workplace, and what a violation costs.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> DO 13 supplies the technical standards; RA 11058 supplies the teeth — the daily fine, the work-stoppage order and the right of a worker to refuse unsafe work.

**What DO 13 is.** Department Order No. 13, issued by the Secretary of Labor and Employment in 1998 under the rule-making authority of the Labor Code (Articles 5, 162 and 168–171, Title I, Book Four), is the **Occupational Safety and Health Standards (OSHS)** — the consolidated technical rulebook for Philippine workplaces. The 1998 issuance is the base text, since amended by later department orders including the 2013 amendments, and the frequently examined title is the **Occupational Safety and Health Standards (as amended)** rather than a bare “OSHA” (that abbreviation belongs to the United States statute). Two Department Orders issued in 2011 to implement it are worth remembering by their content rather than by number: one requires the safety and health programme in the workplace, and the other prescribes the training and the categories of safety officer. The modern statutory overlay is **RA 11058** (approved 17 August 2018, effective fifteen days after publication), which “strengthens compliance with occupational safety and health standards and provides penalties for violations thereof” and is implemented by the DOLE's IRR. Where the two disagree, RA 11058 and its IRR govern.

**Scope and coverage.** RA 11058 §2 applies to all establishments, projects and sites, including Philippine Economic Zone Authority establishments and all other places where work is being undertaken, in all branches of economic activity, **except the public sector**; the Secretary of Labor and Employment determines the number of employees, the nature of operations and the risk or hazard involved, and those factors define a **covered workplace** (§3(c)). Micro enterprises are establishments employing fewer than ten (10) employees and small enterprises fewer than one hundred (100) (§3(h)). DO 13 itself reaches broadly across workplaces, with rule-specific exceptions — for example, the OSH Standards expressly exempt from their coverage and from the need for a safety and health committee those establishments with **fewer than ten (10) employees**, and shops and offices with a low risk classification may be excluded from the committee requirement. The general principle to remember: the *safety-officer and committee* requirements are graduated by manpower size, risk or hazard level and nature of operations, so the number of workers is the first thing a question will vary.

**Employer and worker duties.** RA 11058 §4(a) requires every employer, contractor or subcontractor, and any person who manages, controls or supervises the work, to furnish a place of employment free from hazardous conditions likely to cause death, illness or physical harm; give complete job safety instructions or orientation, especially to those entering the job for the first time, including familiarisation with the work environment; inform workers of the hazards and health risks associated with their work, the preventive measures, and the steps to take in an emergency; use only approved devices and equipment; comply with OSH standards including training, medical examination and, where necessary, the provision of protective and safety devices such as personal protective equipment and machine guards; allow workers and their safety and health representatives to participate actively in organising, planning, implementing and evaluating the safety and health programme; and provide measures to deal with emergencies and accidents, including first-aid arrangements. §4(b) requires every worker to participate in ensuring compliance, make proper use of all safeguards and safety devices furnished for his protection and that of others, observe instructions to prevent accidents or imminent danger, observe prescribed emergency steps, and **report to the supervisor any work hazard discovered**. §4(d) adds that where two or more undertakings work in one workplace, all must collaborate in applying OSH standards. When two or more undertakings work in the same place, the employer, project owner, general contractor, contractor or subcontractor and any person managing or supervising the work are **jointly and solidarily liable** for compliance (§21).

**The committee, the safety officer and the health personnel.** RA 11058 §12 requires every covered workplace to have a safety and health programme containing, among other items, a statement of commitment, general safety and health including a drug-free workplace, HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis and hepatitis prevention and control, company or project details, the composition and duties of the safety and health committee, OSH personnel and facilities, promotion, training and education, toolbox meetings, accident/incident/illness investigation, recording and reporting, provision and use of PPE, safety signage, dust control, welfare facilities, an emergency preparedness and response plan, waste management, and the prohibited acts and penalties — prepared and executed by the employer in consultation with the workers and their representatives and **submitted to the DOLE**. §13 requires a **safety and health committee**, composed of the employer or his representative as chairperson **ex officio**, the safety officer of the company or project as **secretary**, the safety officer representing the contractor or subcontractor as a member, the physicians, nurses, certified first-aiders and dentists **ex officio** if applicable, and workers' representatives who come from the union if the workers are organised or are elected by the workers through a simple majority vote if they are unorganised. §14 requires **safety officers**, whose number and qualification must be proportionate to the total number of workers and equipment, the size of the work area and other criteria prescribed by the DOLE, and who must oversee the overall management of the safety and health programme, frequently monitor and inspect any health or safety aspect of the operation, assist government inspectors during inspections and accident investigations, and **issue work stoppage orders when necessary**; in the case of a contractor or subcontractor a safety officer must be deployed at each specific area of operations for its own workforce. §15 requires qualified occupational health personnel — physicians, nurses, certified first-aiders and dentists — with supplies, equipment and facilities proportionate to the number of workers and the risk, at ratios prescribed by the DOLE.

**Training and protective equipment.** RA 11058 §16 makes training mandatory on three levels: all safety and health personnel must undergo the mandatory basic occupational safety and health training for safety officers as prescribed by DOLE; **all workers** must undergo the mandatory **eight (8) hours** safety and health seminar required by DOLE, including a portion on joint employer-employee orientation; and all personnel engaged in the operation, erection and dismantling of equipment and scaffolds, structural erection, excavation, blasting, demolition, confined spaces, hazardous chemicals, welding and flame cutting must undergo **specialised instruction and training** on those activities. This last list is the practical answer to “when is a safety permit or specialised training required?” — high-risk operations of that kind carry their own instruction, and DO 13 requires the corresponding permit or clearance for hazardous work. On PPE, §8 requires every employer, contractor or subcontractor to provide workers, **free of charge**, protective equipment for eyes, face, hands and feet, lifeline, safety belt or harness, gas or dust respirators or masks, and protective shields whenever necessary by reason of the hazardous work process or environment, chemical, radiological, mechanical and other irritants or hazards capable of causing injury or impairment through absorption, inhalation or physical contact; all PPE must be of the appropriate type as tested and approved by DOLE based on its standards, and usage must be based on the evaluation and recommendation of the safety officer. The total cost of implementing a duly approved safety and health programme is an integral part of operations cost and must be a **separate pay item** in construction and in all contracting or subcontracting arrangements (§20).

**Enforcement and penalties.** RA 11058 §22 preserves the visitorial power under Article 128 of the Labor Code: the Secretary of Labor and Employment or authorised representatives may enter workplaces at any time of the day or night where work is being performed, examine records and investigate facts to determine compliance, and no person may obstruct, impede, delay or render ineffective their orders, nor may a lower court issue an injunction or restraining order over such enforcement orders. The Secretary may order **stoppage of work or suspension of operations** of any unit or department when noncompliance poses grave and imminent danger to the health and safety of workers; and if the stoppage is due to the employer's violation or fault, the employer must **pay the workers their wages during the period of stoppage** (§23). Penalties sit in §28: wilful failure or refusal to comply with the required OSH standards or with a compliance order makes the employer, contractor or subcontractor liable for an administrative fine **not exceeding ₱100,000 per day** until the violation is corrected, counted from the date the employer or contractor is notified of the violation or the date the compliance order is served, with the amount depending on the frequency or gravity of the violation or the damage caused, and the maximum imposable only when the violation exposes workers to a risk of death, serious injury or serious illness; on top of that daily fine, a separate fine of up to ₱100,000 applies to an employer who aids, conceals or facilitates noncompliance through repeated obstruction or delay in giving DOLE access to the workplace or to relevant records, misrepresentation about adherence to OSH standards in any material aspect, or retaliatory measures such as termination, refusal to pay, reduction of wages and benefits, or discrimination against a worker who gave information relative to the inspection. The DOLE issues the list of offences with corresponding fines depending on severity, frequency and damage, without prejudice to criminal or civil cases; fines collected fund OSH training and programmes. Alongside the fine schedule, RA 11058 gives workers the **right to know** (§5), the **right to refuse unsafe work** where DOLE determines an imminent danger exists and corrective action has not been undertaken, without threat or reprisal (§6), and the **right to report** accidents, dangerous occurrences and hazards to the employer, DOLE and other competent agencies (§7). The institutional actors are the **DOLE regional offices**, which conduct the inspections and issue compliance and work-stoppage orders, and the **Bureau of Working Conditions**, which develops the OSH standards, the training rules and the safety-officer categories; chartered cities with adequate facilities and competent personnel may be allowed to conduct industrial safety inspections within their jurisdiction in coordination with DOLE, subject to national standards (§22).

## Traps & Exam Notes

- Calling the rulebook “OSHA.” The Philippine issuance is the **Occupational Safety and Health Standards (OSHS)** under DOLE Department Order No. 13, s. 1998, as amended; OSHA is the United States statute and agency.
- Applying RA 11058 to government workplaces. Section 2 covers all establishments, projects and sites including PEZA establishments but expressly **excludes the public sector**.
- Making the safety officer chair the OSH committee. RA 11058 §13 makes the employer or his representative the chairperson **ex officio** and the safety officer of the company or project the **secretary**; only a contractor's safety officer sits as an ordinary member.
- Answering that the 8-hour requirement applies only to safety officers. RA 11058 §16(b) makes the eight (8) hour safety and health seminar mandatory for **all workers**; safety and health personnel take the basic OSH training for safety officers instead.
- Treating the ₱100,000 as a one-off fine. The §28(a) fine is **per day until the violation is corrected**, counted from notice or service of the compliance order, with the maximum imposable only where the violation exposes workers to risk of death, serious injury or serious illness.
- Saying only the employer pays for PPE or shares its cost with workers. RA 11058 §8 requires the employer, contractor or subcontractor to provide PPE **free of charge**, and §20 makes the safety and health programme cost a separate pay item in construction and contracting.
- Quoting a remembered safety-officer ratio such as one officer per twenty workers without checking the risk classification. RA 11058 §14 states the rule as proportionate to the total number of workers and equipment, the size of the work area and other DOLE criteria — the DOLE prescribes the ratios, so a question that needs a number will provide it.
- Confusing the safety officer's work-stoppage order with the Secretary's stoppage order, and forgetting the wages consequence. A safety officer may issue work stoppage orders when necessary (§14(d)); only a Secretary-ordered stoppage for imminent danger caused by the employer's fault carries the duty to pay wages during the stoppage (§23).
