# 01 — RA 9292 Scope of Practice

> [!abstract] Scope
> Who may lawfully do which electronics work in the Philippines, what each of the three registrant categories may sign, seal or supervise, and what violating that boundary costs.

## Core Concept

> [!tip] Intuition
> RA 9292 draws one line through the profession: the ECT does non-engineering work, the ECE does engineering work, and only the PECE may consult and seal. Job titles, employer practice and years of experience do not move that line — the statute does.

**One law, three registrant categories.** RA 9292, the *Electronics Engineering Law of 2004* (approved 17 April 2004, effective 15 days after publication), repealed RA 5734, the old Electronics and Communications Engineering Act, and created exactly three categories of registrant: the **Professional Electronics Engineer (PECE)**, the **Electronics Engineer (ECE)** and the **Electronics Technician (ECT)** (Sec. 4). The law defines *electronics* broadly as the science dealing with the development and application of devices and systems involving the flow of electrons or other carriers of electric charge — in vacuum, gas, plasma or semiconductors — including optical, electromagnetic and other energy forms once transduced or converted into electronic signals (Sec. 3(a)).

**The ECE scope is deliberately broad.** Sec. 5(a) covers any work or activity applying engineering sciences and principles to the *investigation, analysis, synthesis, planning, design, specification, research and development, provision, procurement, marketing and sales, manufacture and production, construction and installation, tests/measurements/control, operation, repair, servicing, technical support and maintenance* of electronic components, devices, products, apparatus, instruments, equipment, systems, networks, operations and processes — in electronics, communications and telecommunications, ICT, computers and networking with hardware/firmware/software development, broadcast, cable and wireless television, consumer and industrial electronics, electro-optics/photonics/opto-electronics, electromagnetics, avionics, aerospace, navigational and military applications, medical electronics, robotics, cybernetics, biometrics, and all related and convergent fields. It expressly includes the administration, management, supervision and regulatory aspects of that work, and teaching or training in the electronics engineering and electronics technician curricula and licensure examinations. Board exam questions that ask whether some modern or hybrid field is 'electronics engineering' under Philippine law are answered by this list, not by intuition.

**The PECE owns two things the ECE does not.** Sec. 5(b) gives the PECE everything in Sec. 5(a) *plus the sole authority* to (i) provide **consulting services** as defined in Sec. 3(m) — advisory and review, pre-investment or feasibility studies, design, planning, construction, supervision, management and related or special technical studies — and (ii) **sign and seal** electronics plans, drawings, permit applications, specifications, reports and other technical documents prepared by himself or under his direct supervision. The ECE/PECE boundary is therefore a boundary of *statutory authority*, not of competence: a brilliant ECE with 30 years of design experience still may not seal.

**The ECT scope is defined by exclusion.** Sec. 5(c) covers any **non-engineering** work or activity relating to the installation, construction, operation, control, tests and measurements, diagnosis, repair and maintenance, manufacture and production, sales and marketing of electronic components, devices, products, apparatus, instruments, equipment, systems, networks, operations and processes located on land, watercraft, aircraft, industrial plants or commercial establishments, plus teaching technical subjects in the technician curriculum. That single word 'non-engineering' is the whole test an examiner applies to a scenario about a technician.

**How the boundary is enforced.** Sec. 27 makes it unlawful to hold oneself out as a PECE, ECE or ECT, or to advertise that one is qualified to perform their work, without a valid Certificate of Registration and a valid Professional Identification Card. Sec. 28 bars the registration or licensing of any firm, company, partnership, association or corporation for the practice — the authority is personal to the individual registrant, and members of a partnership or corporation remain responsible for their own respective acts. Sec. 29 requires every PECE to use a Board-prescribed seal bearing his name, registration number and title, stamped on every sheet of any plan, drawing, permit application, specification or report filed with government or used professionally, together with his current Professional Tax Receipt number, date and place of payment, and current accredited-professional-organization membership number. Sec. 34 requires that within three years from effectivity, government positions primarily requiring a PECE, ECE or ECT be filled only by registrants. Sec. 35 penalises nine enumerated acts with a fine of not less than ₱100,000 nor more than ₱1,000,000, or imprisonment of not less than 6 months nor more than 6 years, or both, at the court's discretion.

**Becoming a PECE is an experience-based upgrade, not an examination.** Under Sec. 18 an applicant submits a valid ECE Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card, a valid current accredited-professional-organization membership card, a certified experience record of at least **seven (7) years inclusive or aggregate** of active self-practice or employment in government or private practice — **at least two (2) years of which must be in responsible charge of significant engineering work**, counted from the date he took his oath — and **three (3) certifications signed by three PECEs** attesting that the experience record is factual. The Board then conducts an *en banc* oral interview to assess competency. There is no PECE licensure examination.

## Derivation

**Step 1 — classify the activity by statute, not by job title.** Read the task against Sec. 5(a) and Sec. 5(c). Investigation, analysis, synthesis, planning, design, specification, R&D, testing/measurement/control and the administration or supervision of that work are ECE-level functions under Sec. 5(a). Only the word 'non-engineering' describes the ECT scope in Sec. 5(c): installation, operation, control, test and measurement, diagnosis, repair, maintenance, production, sales and marketing, and technician-level teaching.

**Step 2 — ask whether the deliverable is a consulting service or a document that must be signed and sealed.** If it is, Sec. 5(b) reserves it to the PECE. This is a binary statutory test: the answer does not depend on the size, risk or complexity of the project, nor on the experience of the ECE who prepared it.

**Step 3 — identify the person who holds the authority, not the entity.** Under Sec. 28 no firm, company, partnership, association or corporation may be registered or licensed for the practice of electronics engineering or electronics technology. Registrants may form a partnership, association or corporation and render services collectively, but each individual member answers for his own acts. A company therefore satisfies the law only by employing a PECE who personally seals under Sec. 29.

**Step 4 — test the status of the licence and the document.** Sec. 27 requires a *valid* Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card; Sec. 29 requires the seal to carry the PECE's name, registration number, title, current PTR number and current organization membership number. Sealing after revocation or suspension, or signing work one did not actually perform, is criminal under Sec. 35(e)–(g) even for a person who was once duly registered.

**Step 5 — reconcile a titled 'Electronics and Communications Engineer' registered under the old law.** Sec. 42 repeals RA 5734, and Sec. 38 converts every RA 5734 registrant with a valid certificate and ID into an **Electronics Engineer** with a new certificate bearing the same licence number, on payment of the prescribed fees. The conversion is to ECE, never to PECE, so the sealing authority of Sec. 5(b) is unaffected.

## Worked Problems

### P1. A developer needs the electronics plans and permit application for a building signed and sealed. The design was prepared in-house by a registered ECE. Can the ECE sign and seal it, and if not, who can?

**Given:** preparer: registered ECE; deliverable: electronic plans and permit application; question: who holds sealing authority

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 5(a) puts design, planning and specification within the ECE scope, so preparing the plans is lawful for the ECE
2. Sec. 5(b) gives the PECE, and only the PECE, the sole authority to sign and seal electronics plans, drawings, permit applications, specifications, reports and other technical documents prepared by himself or under his direct supervision
3. Sealing is therefore outside the ECE's statutory scope regardless of experience; the document must be signed and sealed by a PECE
4. Sec. 29 additionally requires the seal to carry the PECE's name, registration number and title, with his current PTR number and organization membership number on every sheet

> [!success]- Answer
> **No. A registered ECE may prepare the plans but may not sign or seal them; only a PECE may, under Sec. 5(b) and Sec. 29.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Assuming the most senior or most experienced ECE on staff can seal. Sec. 5(b) makes sealing a matter of registrant category, not seniority; a non-PECE ECE who seals commits an act punishable under Sec. 35.

### P2. An ECE registered in 2015 has six (6) years of continuous design employment. He applies for registration as a PECE. Decide the application.

**Given:** valid ECE Certificate of Registration and ID; 6 years of continuous practice; target: PECE registration under Sec. 18

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 18 requires a valid ECE certificate and ID — satisfied
2. Sec. 18(b) requires a valid, current accredited-professional-organization membership card, and Sec. 18(c) requires a certified experience record of at least 7 years inclusive or aggregate, counted from the date of oath
3. The applicant has only 6 years, so the 7-year threshold is not met; the 2-years-in-responsible-charge sub-requirement cannot be assessed until it is
4. Sec. 18(d) further requires 3 certifications signed by 3 PECEs, and the Board conducts an en banc oral interview before registration

> [!success]- Answer
> **Deny — the applicant falls short of the mandatory 7-year experience requirement of Sec. 18(c). Reapplication is possible once the requirement is met.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Treating PECE registration as a promotion granted after 'enough' experience, or as an examination to be passed. It is a statutory experience threshold of 7 years (with at least 2 years in responsible charge) verified by 3 PECE certifications and an en banc interview.

### P3. A corporation applies to the Commission for a certificate to engage in the practice of electronics engineering, offering to employ registered ECEs and PECEs as its technical staff. May it be registered or licensed?

**Given:** applicant: corporation; staff: registered ECEs and PECEs; question: registrability of the entity

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 28 states that the practice of electronics engineering and electronics technology is a professional service whose admission must be determined on the basis of the individual's personal qualifications
2. It follows that no firm, company, partnership, association or corporation may be registered or licensed as such for the practice
3. Registered PECEs, ECEs or ECTs may form a partnership, association or corporation with each other or with allied professionals and render services collectively
4. But the proviso makes the individual members responsible for their own respective acts, so the corporate veil does not carry the licence

> [!success]- Answer
> **No. The corporation cannot be registered or licensed for the practice (Sec. 28); the licence belongs to the individual registrants, who may practise collectively but each answer for their own acts.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Answering 'yes, provided it employs a PECE'. Employment satisfies the sealing requirement of Sec. 29, but Sec. 28 still forbids licensing the entity itself — the registrant, not the company, holds the authority.

### P4. An electronics technician (ECT) employed by a manufacturing plant prepares and seals the plant's equipment layout drawings. He argues that he has done the work for 12 years and no ECE is available. Is the sealing lawful?

**Given:** registrant: ECT; act: preparing and sealing equipment layout drawings; defence: 12 years of experience, no ECE available

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 5(c) confines the ECT scope to non-engineering work or activity — installation, construction, operation, control, tests and measurements, diagnosis, repair, maintenance, production, sales and technician-level teaching
2. Sealing is reserved by Sec. 5(b) to the PECE, so an ECT has no sealing authority at all
3. Experience and the absence of an ECE are not statutory exceptions; the only exemptions in the Act concern temporarily employed foreign professionals under Sec. 26
4. Sec. 35(d) punishes assuming or practising a title one does not hold, and Sec. 35(e) punishes stamping or sealing a document after suspension, revocation or removal from the roster

> [!success]- Answer
> **No. An ECT may not seal; sealing is PECE-only under Sec. 5(b), and doing it exposes the technician to the penalties of Sec. 35.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Accepting the 'no ECE available' justification. RA 9292 provides no shortage exception for domestic practice — the only exemptions are the narrow foreign-professional special permits of Sec. 26.

### P5. An unlicensed person advertises on social media as an 'ECE' and offers electronics system design services. What offence is committed and what is the penalty?

**Given:** person: not registered with the PRC; act: using the title ECE and advertising the work; question: offence and penalty

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 27 makes it unlawful to offer oneself as, or use the title PECE, ECE or ECT, or to advertise qualification to perform their work, without a valid Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card
2. Sec. 35(d) punishes any person who assumes, uses, advertises or otherwise practises as a PECE, ECE or ECT, or appends such letters to his name, when not duly registered
3. Sec. 35 sets one penalty range for all nine enumerated acts: a fine of not less than ₱100,000 nor more than ₱1,000,000, or imprisonment of not less than 6 months nor more than 6 years, or both, in the court's discretion

> [!success]- Answer
> **Unlicensed practice and use of the title under Sec. 27 and Sec. 35(d), punishable by a fine of ₱100,000 to ₱1,000,000, or imprisonment of 6 months to 6 years, or both.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Quoting only a fine, or a fine below ₱100,000. Sec. 35 requires a fine of at least ₱100,000, and the court may impose imprisonment instead of or in addition to the fine.

### P6. An ECE board candidate obtains 78%, 74%, 71%, 70% and 65% in the five subjects, passing the majority. What is his status and what happens if he fails the removal examination?

**Given:** ratings: 78, 74, 71, 70 and 65 percent; majority of subjects passed; one subject below 70 but not below 60

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 16 requires a passing rating of 70% in each subject given during the examination, so the 65% subject is a failure
2. The proviso allows one removal examination where the candidate passed the majority of the subjects and the failed subject(s) are below 70% but not lower than 60%
3. The candidate passed four of five subjects and the remaining rating is 65%, which is within 60–70%, so a removal examination is available
4. Should he fail to obtain a passing rating in the removal examination, Sec. 16 provides that he is considered as having failed the entire licensure examination

> [!success]- Answer
> **He is allowed one removal examination on the failed subject; failing that removal, he is deemed to have failed the entire licensure examination.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Applying a 70% general average. Sec. 16 requires 70% in each subject, and a rating below 60% in any subject removes the removal-examination privilege altogether.

### P7. A foreign PECE is contracted for a one-year telecom project in the Philippines. What must be obtained, for how long, and what local staffing condition applies?

**Given:** foreign PECE, temporarily employed; project duration: 1 year; framework: Sec. 26 exemptions

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 26 exempts foreign professionals from examination and registration only where no qualified equivalent Filipino professional is available as attested by the accredited professional organization, where the project stipulates temporary foreign employment, or under GATS and the ASEAN/APEC Engineer Registry programmes
2. Before commencing work the foreigner must obtain a Special Permit from the Board, subject to approval of the Commission, and no working visa or permit may issue before that permit is granted
3. The permit is valid for not more than 6 months and is renewable every 6 months at the Board's discretion with the Commission's approval; the work is limited to the contracted project and no private practice on his own account is allowed
4. For every foreign professional contracted, at least two (2) corresponding Filipino registrants must be employed as counterparts for at least the same duration

> [!success]- Answer
> **A Board-issued Special Permit with PRC approval, valid for not more than 6 months and renewable every 6 months, plus at least two Filipino counterparts employed for at least the same duration; no private practice on his own account.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Assuming the permit covers the whole one-year project, or that the foreigner may open his own consultancy. The permit is capped at 6 months renewable, is tied to the specific project, and expressly excludes private practice.

### P8. An engineer holds an 'Electronics and Communications Engineer' certificate issued in 2002 under RA 5734. What is his status after RA 9292 took effect, and may he seal plans?

**Given:** valid RA 5734 certificate and ID at effectivity; Act effective 15 days after publication (Sec. 43); question: converted category and sealing authority

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 42 repeals RA 5734 and all inconsistent laws
2. Sec. 38 provides that Electronics and Communications Engineers holding a valid Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card at effectivity are automatically registered and recognised as Electronics Engineers
3. They are issued a new certificate and ID as Electronics Engineers bearing the same licence number, subject to prescribed fees and other requirements
4. The conversion is to ECE, and sealing authority under Sec. 5(b) belongs only to the PECE; a former ECE title-holder may register as a PECE only by satisfying Sec. 18

> [!success]- Answer
> **He is automatically an Electronics Engineer (ECE) with the same licence number; he may not seal until he qualifies as a PECE under Sec. 18.**

> [!warning] Trap
> Assuming the 2002 registrant becomes a PECE, or that his old 'ECE' title survives. RA 5734 is repealed; the vested right created by Sec. 38 is conversion to Electronics Engineer, not elevation to PECE.

### P9. A PECE is asked to seal a report prepared entirely by another PECE, and separately a colleague whose certificate was suspended last month continues to stamp drawings. Are these violations?

**Given:** case A: sealing a report prepared by another PECE; case B: sealing after suspension; question: exposure under Sec. 35

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 35(f) punishes a PECE who signs, seals or otherwise signs plans, technical descriptions or other documents prepared by or under the supervision of another PECE unless the document clearly indicates the part of the work actually performed by the former
2. In case A the report was prepared entirely by another PECE, so attempting to seal it as one's own work without such indication falls within Sec. 35(f)
3. Sec. 35(e) punishes stamping or sealing any document after the Certificate of Registration, Professional ID and organisation membership card have been revoked or suspended, or after suspension from practice
4. Case B is squarely within Sec. 35(e); Sec. 23 also authorises the Board, after notice and hearing, to revoke or suspend the certificate for unprofessional conduct or violation of the Act

> [!success]- Answer
> **Both are violations: case A under Sec. 35(f) unless the extent of work performed is clearly indicated, case B under Sec. 35(e).**

> [!warning] Trap
> Believing that a co-signature between two PECEs is routine and lawful. Sec. 35(f) permits it only where the document clearly shows which part of the work each signer actually performed.

### P10. State the three statutory time limits tested most often under RA 9292: how long the window for ECT registration without examination lasted, when government positions had to be filled by registrants, and when the Act itself took effect.

**Given:** Act approved 17 April 2004; three separate time limits; Secs. 20, 34 and 43

**Solution:**

1. Sec. 20 gave the Board five (5) years after effectivity to issue certificates and IDs without examination to ECT applicants who were graduates of at least a two-year TESDA-certified electronics course and had at least 7 years of practice plus 3 PECE certifications
2. Sec. 34 required that within three (3) years from effectivity, existing and proposed local and national government positions primarily requiring a PECE, ECE or ECT be filled only by registered and licensed registrants
3. Sec. 43 provides that the Act takes effect fifteen (15) days following its full publication in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation
4. Sec. 17 separately requires examination results to be released within fifteen (15) days, and Sec. 7(t) allows fifteen (15) days to appeal a Board decision to the Commission

> [!success]- Answer
> **Five (5) years for ECT registration without examination (Sec. 20); three (3) years for government positions (Sec. 34); fifteen (15) days after publication for effectivity (Sec. 43).**

> [!warning] Trap
> Mixing up the two 7-year rules and the several 15-day rules: seven years is the PECE experience requirement (Sec. 18) *and* the ECT registration-without-examination practice requirement (Sec. 20), while fifteen days applies to effectivity, to the release of results and to the appeal to the Commission.

## Traps & Exam Notes

- **Answering that any ECE may sign and seal plans.** Sec. 5(b) reserves the sole authority to provide consulting services and to sign and seal to the PECE. A registered ECE's Sec. 5(a) scope is broad but stops short of sealing, and sealing without that authority is punishable under Sec. 35.
- **Assuming a 70% general average passes the board.** Sec. 16 requires 70% **in each subject**; the removal examination is available only when the candidate passed the majority of subjects and the failed subject(s) are between 60% and 70%.
- **Quoting the wrong penalty range or form.** Sec. 35 sets a fine of ₱100,000 to ₱1,000,000 **or** imprisonment of 6 months to 6 years **or both** — the court may imprison *and* fine, and no fine below ₱100,000 is authorised.
- **Thinking a firm, partnership or corporation can be registered for practice.** Sec. 28 forbids it outright; registrants may associate and render services collectively, but each member remains responsible for his own acts.
- **Treating the old 'Electronics and Communications Engineer' title as still existing.** RA 5734 is repealed (Sec. 42) and its registrants are converted to **Electronics Engineer** with the same licence number (Sec. 38) — never automatically to PECE.
- **Confusing the two 7-year requirements.** Seven years of experience is needed for PECE registration (Sec. 18, with at least 2 years in responsible charge) and seven years of practice was needed for ECT registration without examination (Sec. 20). They belong to different categories and different provisions.
- **Forgetting the Filipino-counterpart rule for foreign professionals.** A Sec. 26 special permit is capped at 6 months and renewable, is limited to the contracted project, bars private practice on the foreigner's own account, and requires at least two Filipino registrants employed as counterparts for at least the same duration.
- **Assuming a suspended or revoked registrant may keep signing.** Sec. 35(e) makes stamping or sealing after revocation or suspension an offence, and Sec. 24 requires a two-year wait before the Board may reinstate a revoked certificate.
