# 02 — Periodic Trends

> [!abstract] Scope
> Rank atoms and ions by size, ionisation energy, electron affinity and metallic character, and justify each ranking from nuclear charge and shielding.

## Formulas

| Quantity | Expression | Notes |
| --- | :---: | --- |
| Effective nuclear charge | $Z_{\mathrm{eff}} = Z - S$ | S is shielding, dominated by core electrons. Across a period Z rises while S barely moves, so Z_eff rises and the atom contracts. |
| Atomic radius trend | $r \downarrow \mathrm{\ left\ to\ right}; \quad r \uparrow \mathrm{\ top\ to\ bottom}$ | Driven by Z_eff, not by electron count. A group going down adds a whole shell, so the increase always wins. |
| Isoelectronic series radius | $\mathrm{more\ positive\ charge} \Rightarrow \mathrm{smaller\ radius}$ | Same electron count only. O2- > F- > Ne > Na+ > Mg2+; comparing across different electron counts is meaningless. |
| First ionisation energy | $\mathrm{X}(g) \to \mathrm{X^+}(g) + e^-, \quad IE_1$ | Always gaseous and always endothermic. IE_2 > IE_1; only IE_1 follows the smooth periodic trend. |
| Successive IE signature | $\mathrm{large\ jump} \Rightarrow \mathrm{core\ electron\ reached}$ | A jump after the 2nd electron means 2 valence electrons (group 2); after the 7th it means group 17. |
| Electron affinity | $\mathrm{X}(g) + e^- \to \mathrm{X^-}(g), \quad EA$ | Usually exothermic. EA(Cl) = -349 kJ/mol is MORE negative than EA(F) = -328 kJ/mol because F's compact 2p subshell repels the incoming electron. |
| Pauling electronegativity | $\chi: \mathrm{F}=3.98 > \mathrm{O}=3.44 > \mathrm{Cl}=3.16 > \mathrm{N}=3.04 > \mathrm{C}=2.55$ | Fluorine is the maximum. Values are dimensionless and relative, so only differences matter. |
| Bond polarity from electronegativity | $\Delta\chi = \lvert \chi_A - \chi_B \rvert$ | A rough cut: below about 0.4 largely covalent, above about 1.7 largely ionic. It is a continuum, not a boundary. |
| Metallic character | $\mathrm{metallic} \uparrow \mathrm{\ down-left}; \quad \mathrm{nonmetallic} \uparrow \mathrm{\ up-right}$ | Caesium is the most metallic stable element; exam keys usually want Cs rather than the rarer, radioactive francium. |
| Diagonal relationship | $\mathrm{Li}\sim\mathrm{Mg}, \quad \mathrm{Be}\sim\mathrm{Al}, \quad \mathrm{B}\sim\mathrm{Si}$ | Similar charge-to-radius ratio gives similar chemistry (both Li and Mg form nitrides, both Be and Al are amphoteric). |

## Traps & Exam Notes

- **Ranking ionisation energy by shell number alone.** Across period 3, $IE_1$ climbs from Na to Ar but dips at Al and at S: Al's third electron is removed from the higher-energy 3p, and S's from an already-paired 3p where repulsion assists removal. A clean left-to-right answer is wrong at exactly those two points.
- **Ranking radius by electron count.** Cations are smaller and anions larger than their parent atom even though only the electron count changed; $\mathrm{Na^+}$ (10 e⁻) is smaller than $\mathrm{Cl^-}$ (18 e⁻) because $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ dominates. Any ranking built on 'more electrons means bigger' inverts.
- **Assuming the top-right element has the largest electron affinity.** It does not: $EA(\mathrm{Cl}) = -349\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$ beats $EA(\mathrm{F}) = -328\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}$ because fluorine's compact 2p subshell has strong inter-electron repulsion. Fluorine still wins on electronegativity, which is a different quantity.
- **Treating shielding as a full electron count.** Only core electrons shield efficiently; same-shell electrons shield poorly. That is why $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rises steeply across a period even though Z and the electron count each rise by one per element.
