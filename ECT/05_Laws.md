# 05 — Laws and Ethics (deep)

Statutory content below is taken from **RA 9292 as enacted** (approved 17 April 2004). PEC content is written at the standard NEC-derived level that the Philippine Electrical Code adopts; where a figure depends on the **edition cited**, answer from the citation in the question.

## 1. Where each document gets its force

```
RA 9292 (statute)  ──►  its IRR  ──►  Code of Ethics + Code of Technical Standards of Practice
                                          │
                                          ├─► Philippine Electronics Code  (Board of Electronics Eng'g)
                                          └─► rules for electronics installations in buildings (with DPWH)

Electrical practice law ──► Philippine Electrical Code (Board of Electrical Engineering)

PD 1096 National Building Code ──► adopts referral codes ──► permits, inspection
```

| Document | Issuer | Legal character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RA 9292** | Congress | Statute; creates the Board, the three categories, the penalties |
| IRR of RA 9292 | Board, with Commission approval | Rules implementing the statute |
| **Code of Ethics / Code of Technical Standards of Practice** | Board (RA 9292 §7(12)) | Enforceable through §35(9) |
| **Philippine Electronics Code** | Board of Electronics Engineering | Technical code under §7(16) and §39 |
| **Philippine Electrical Code** | Board of Electrical Engineering | Technical code for electrical installations |
| **PD 1096** (National Building Code) | Congress, implemented by DPWH | Adopts the referral codes, which is how the technical codes bite |

The examinable hierarchy: **a code is not a statute**. It takes its force from the law that authorises it, is published, and is **effective 15 days after publication** (§39).

## 2. RA 9292 — section map

### Article I — General provisions

| Section | Content                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| §1      | Short title: **Electronics Engineering Law of 2004**                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| §2      | Policy: competent, virtuous PECEs, ECEs, ECTs through honest and credible licensure examinations                                                                                                                                                                  |
| §3      | **14 definitions.** Key ones: electronics; PECE; ECE; ECT; ECE under RA 5734; computer; ICT; communications; telecommunications; broadcast; **industrial plant**; **commercial establishment**; **consulting services**; **accredited professional organization** |
| §4      | **Three categories only**: PECE, ECE, ECT                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| §5      | Scope of practice, split three ways                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

**Definitions worth quoting:**

- **Electronics** (§3(1)): the science dealing with devices and systems involving the flow of electrons or other carriers of charge, *in vacuum, in gaseous media, in plasma, in semiconductors, in solid-state*, including optical and electromagnetic applications *when transduced or converted into electronic signals*.
- **Industrial plant** (§3(11)): manufacturing establishments and other businesses where electronic or electronically-controlled machinery *are installed, used, sold, maintained, assembled, manufactured or operated*.
- **Commercial establishment** (§3(12)): office buildings, hotels, motels, hospitals, condominiums, stores, apartments, supermarkets, schools, studios, stadia, parking areas, memorial chapels/parks, **watercraft and aircraft used for business or profit**.
- **Consulting services** (§3(13)): advisory and review, pre-investment/feasibility studies, design, planning, construction, supervision, management, and special technical studies.

The definitions matter because §7(15) lets the Board **prescribe minimum manning in industrial plants and commercial establishments** — so those two defined terms are live law, not background.

### Article I — §5, the three scopes

| Category | Scope | May sign and seal? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ECE** | Engineering work: investigation, analysis, synthesis, planning, **design**, specification, R&D, provision, procurement, marketing and sales, manufacture and production, construction and installation, tests/measurements/control, operation, repair, servicing, technical support and maintenance of electronic components, devices, products, apparatus, instruments, equipment, systems and networks — across electronics, communications/telecommunications, **ICT**, computers and networking with hardware/firmware/software development, **broadcast**, cable and wireless television, consumer and industrial electronics, electro-optics/photonics/opto-electronics, electromagnetics, **avionics, aerospace, navigational and military applications, medical electronics, robotics, cybernetics, biometrics** and convergent fields; plus administration, management, supervision and regulation of such work, and teaching in the ECE/ECT curriculum and licensure examinations | **No** |
| **PECE** | All of the ECE scope **plus** the *sole authority* to provide **consulting services** and to **sign and seal** electronics plans, drawings, permit applications, specifications, reports and other technical documents prepared by himself or **under his direct supervision** | **Yes — sole** |
| **ECT** | Any **non-engineering** work or activity relating to installation, construction, operation, control, test and measurements, diagnosis, repair and maintenance, manufacture and production, sales and marketing of electronic components, devices, products, apparatus, instruments, equipment, systems, networks, operations and processes located **on land, watercraft, aircraft, industrial plants or commercial establishments**, including teaching technical and professional subjects in the technician curriculum and licensure examinations | **No** |

**The ECT test is the word "non-engineering."** Every scenario item about a technician resolves on it: installation yes, diagnosis yes, repair yes, **design no, consulting no, sealing no**. Seniority, employer policy and absence of an engineer are not exceptions.

### §35 — the nine penalised acts

One penalty range covers all nine:

> **Fine ₱100,000 – ₱1,000,000, or imprisonment 6 months – 6 years, or both, in the court's discretion.**

| Letter | Act |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | False or fraudulent statement to obtain a certificate/PIC |
| (b) | Using another's certificate, PIC, APO card or **seal** — and allowing such use |
| (c) | Using a **revoked or suspended** certificate |
| (d) | **Assuming, using, advertising or practising** as PECE/ECE/ECT while not registered, or appending the letters |
| (e) | Stamping or sealing **after** revocation, suspension or removal from the roster |
| (f) | A PECE sealing work prepared by another PECE **without clearly indicating the part actually performed** |
| (g) | Anyone other than the PECE/ECE in charge signing for electronics work **not actually performed** by him |
| (h) | Illegal wiretapping, cloning, hacking, cracking, piracy, unauthorised electronic eavesdropping, or **maintaining an unlicensed/unregistered communications system** |
| (i) | Any violation of the Act, its rules, the **Code of Ethics** or the **Code of Technical Standards of Practice** |

Subsection **(h)** is the one technicians overlook: it is a criminal offence for a *registered* professional to maintain an unregistered communications system — directly relevant to installing radio equipment.

**Two penalty pairs that are constantly swapped:**

| Offence | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| RA 9292 §35 (unlicensed practice, sealing without authority, etc.) | ₱100k–₱1M **or** 6 months–6 years **or both** |
| RA 8981 §15 (rigging exams, leaking questions, tampering grades) | **6 years and 1 day – 12 years** or ₱50k–₱100k, or both; a board member or PRC employee also suffers removal and perpetual disqualification |

## 3. Philippine Electrical Code — working depth

The PEC is the **electrical wiring and installation standard**, adopted as a referral code of the National Building Code and enforced through local building officials and electrical inspectors. It follows the **NEC** structure with Philippine amendments, so the exam figures are the familiar NEC tables.

### Structure worth knowing

| Chapter | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | General: definitions, requirements for electrical installations |
| 2 | Wiring and protection: **branch circuits, feeders, services, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection** |
| 3 | Wiring methods and materials: conductors, raceways, boxes, cable trays |
| 4 | Equipment for general use: switches, receptacles, switchboards, motors, air-conditioning |
| 5 | Special occupancies (hazardous locations, health care, etc.) |
| 6 | Special equipment (cranes, elevators, signs, welders) |
| 7 | Special conditions (emergency systems, standby power, low-voltage systems) |
| **8** | **Communications systems** — the technician's own chapter (telephone, CATV, radio/TV, network-powered broadband) |
| 9 | Tables: conductor properties, conduit fill, ampacities |

### Definitions that decide items

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Ampacity** | Current a conductor can carry continuously without exceeding its temperature rating |
| **Branch circuit** | Conductors after the final overcurrent device |
| **Feeder** | Conductors between the service equipment and the final branch-circuit protection |
| **Service** | Conductors and equipment from the utility to the service disconnect |
| **Continuous load** | Load operating **3 hours or more** |
| **Grounded conductor** | The **neutral** (white) — intentionally at ground potential |
| **Grounding conductor** | The **equipment ground** (green/bare) — a fault path, never a current-carrying return |
| **Bonding** | Connecting metal parts so no potential difference can exist between them |
| **GFCI** | Detects a difference between outgoing and returning current, trips at ~**5 mA** |
| **AFCI** | Detects the signature of an arcing fault |

### Ampacity and derating

Table values are for **30 °C ambient** and **not more than 3 current-carrying conductors in a raceway or cable**. Two corrections then apply:

| Ambient | Correction factor (60/75 °C) |
| --- | ---: |
| 31–35 °C | 0.96 |
| 36–40 °C | 0.91 |
| 41–45 °C | 0.87 |
| 46–50 °C | 0.82 |

| Conductors in raceway | Adjustment |
| --- | ---: |
| 1–3 | 1.00 |
| 4–6 | **0.80** |
| 7–9 | 0.70 |
| 10–20 | 0.50 |

**P1 — Derating a conductor for ambient and bundling.**
A **#10 THHN** copper conductor (75 °C column, tabulated ampacity **35 A**) is installed in a raceway containing **6 current-carrying conductors**, in an ambient temperature of **40 °C**.

**Given:** tabulated ampacity = 35 A; ambient 40 °C → correction factor 0.91; 6 conductors in one raceway → adjustment factor 0.80
**Find:** the derated ampacity, and the largest standard breaker that may protect it
**Solution:** both factors multiply the table value:

$$35\ \text{A} \times 0.91 \times 0.80 = 25.5\ \text{A}$$

**Answer:** the conductor may carry **25.5 A**, so it is protected at **25 A**. The table value of 35 A is never the number that sizes the breaker once ambient or bundling applies — and a question that skips the 0.80 factor will answer 32 A and be wrong.

The derated ampacity — not the table value — governs the breaker.

### Continuous loads

- Continuous load **must not exceed 80%** of the device rating.
- The circuit and the **overcurrent device are sized at 125%** of the continuous load.

**P2 — Sizing a branch circuit for a continuous load.**
A commercial lighting circuit carries a continuous load of **16 A** at 230 V. The load runs for more than three hours at a time.

**Given:** continuous load $I = 16\ \text{A}$; continuous-load factor 125%
**Find:** the minimum conductor ampacity and the standard overcurrent device
**Solution:** a continuous load is taken at 125% for both the conductor and the overcurrent device:

$$I_{design} = 1.25 \times 16\ \text{A} = 20\ \text{A}$$

**Answer:** **#12 copper (20 A at 60 °C) on a 20 A breaker.** Using the raw 16 A would put a 15 A breaker on a load that is legal at 80% of 20 A — and would nuisance-trip every few hours.

### Voltage drop

$$V_{drop} = \frac{2KIL}{CM} \quad \text{(single phase, round trip)}$$

$K$ = 12.9 for copper (21.2 for aluminium), $I$ in amps, $L$ in **feet**, $CM$ = circular mils.

| Size | CM |
| --- | ---: |
| 14 | 4110 |
| 12 | 6530 |
| 10 | 10 380 |
| 8 | 16 510 |
| 6 | 26 240 |
| 4 | 41 740 |
| 2 | 66 360 |

**P3 — Voltage drop on a long branch circuit.**
A **120 V**, single-phase branch circuit carries **20 A** through **50 m (164 ft)** of **#10** copper conductor (10 380 circular mils), with $K = 12.9$ for copper.

**Given:** $I = 20\ \text{A}$; one-way length $L = 164\ \text{ft}$; $CM = 10\,380$; $K = 12.9$; source 120 V
**Find:** the voltage drop and its percentage, and whether it meets the 3% branch-circuit limit
**Solution:** the factor 2 accounts for the round trip:

$$V_{drop} = \frac{2KIL}{CM} = \frac{2(12.9)(20\ \text{A})(164\ \text{ft})}{10\,380} = \frac{84\,624}{10\,380} = 8.15\ \text{V}$$

$$\%V_{drop} = \frac{8.15\ \text{V}}{120\ \text{V}} \times 100 = 6.8\%$$

**Answer:** **8.15 V, or 6.8% — it fails** the 3% branch limit. Going up one size to #8 (16 510 CM) gives 5.1 V = 4.3%, still over; the compliant answer is #6 or a shorter run. This is the arithmetic behind "my tool is weak on a long extension cord".

Exceeds the **3% branch-circuit limit**; #8 (16 510 CM) gives 5.1 V = 4.3%, still over for a branch circuit — the real answer is #6 or a shorter run. This is how a "why is my tool weak on a long extension cord" question is answered numerically.

### Box fill

Count each conductor by size; a device yoke counts as **two conductors of the largest size**, all grounds together count as **one**, clamps **one**.

| Conductor | Volume each |
| --- | ---: |
| #14 | 2.00 in³ |
| #12 | 2.25 in³ |
| #10 | 2.50 in³ |

**P4 — Box fill for a switch box.**
A box contains **6 current-carrying #12 conductors**, **one device** (a switch on a yoke), **one equipment grounding conductor** and **internal cable clamps**. Each #12 conductor is allowed **2.25 in³**.

**Given:** 6 conductors at 2.25 in³; a yoke counts as 2 conductors of the largest size; all grounds together count as 1 conductor; clamps count as 1 conductor
**Find:** the minimum box volume, and whether a 4-inch square × 2⅛-inch deep box (30.3 in³) is adequate
**Solution:** count in conductor-equivalents, then multiply by the per-conductor volume:

$$6(2.25) + 2(2.25) + 1(2.25) + 1(2.25) = 13.5 + 4.5 + 2.25 + 2.25 = 22.5\ \text{in}^3$$

**Answer:** the box needs **at least 22.5 in³**; the 30.3 in³ box **is adequate** with 7.8 in³ to spare. The two traps are forgetting that the yoke counts as two conductors and that grounds count as one, not one each.

### Conduit fill

| Conductors | Maximum fill |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 | 53% |
| 2 | 31% |
| **3 or more** | **40%** |

**P5 — How many conductors fit in a raceway.**
A **½-inch EMT** raceway has an internal cross-sectional area of **0.304 in²**. **#12 THHN** conductors, each occupying **0.0133 in²**, are to be pulled through it.

**Given:** raceway internal area = 0.304 in²; fill limit for 3 or more conductors = **40%**; conductor area = 0.0133 in²
**Find:** the maximum number of conductors
**Solution:** permitted area is 40% of the raceway area; divide by the conductor area and round **down**:

$$A_{permitted} = 0.40(0.304\ \text{in}^2) = 0.122\ \text{in}^2 \qquad N = \frac{0.122\ \text{in}^2}{0.0133\ \text{in}^2} = 9.17$$

**Answer:** **9 conductors.** Always round down — 9.17 conductors is not 10 — and note that pulling 9 pushes the derating factor in P1 to 0.70, so the ampacity falls with the count.

### Grounding and bonding

- The **service neutral is bonded to the grounding electrode** at the service only — bonding it again downstream creates parallel neutral current on the ground path.
- The **grounding electrode conductor** is sized from the service conductor (Table 250.66): #2 service or smaller → **#8**; 1/0–3/0 → **#4**; over 3/0–350 kcmil → **#2**.
- Sub-panels get a **separate ground bar**; neutral and ground are **not** bonded there.
- Metal water pipe, building steel, concrete-encased electrode and ground rods are all bonding/grounding electrodes; each has its own minimum size.

### GFCI and AFCI

| Device | Protects against | Where required (typical) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GFCI** | Electric shock — earth leakage, trips ~5 mA | Bathrooms, kitchens (countertop receptacles), outdoors, garages, unfinished basements, near pools, laundry areas |
| **AFCI** | Fires from arcing | Bedrooms and, in later editions, most living areas |

A GFCI does **not** require a ground wire to function — it compares outgoing and returning current. That is why it is accepted as a retrofit protection on ungrounded circuits, marked "no equipment ground".

### Motors

- Branch-circuit conductors: **125% of the table full-load current**.
- Overload protection: **115–125%** of nameplate current.
- Short-circuit protection may be much larger (up to 250% for an inverse-time breaker on some designs) because starting current is 5–8 × FLA.
- The **overload relay protects the motor**; the breaker protects the **conductors**.

### Chapter 8 — communications circuits (the ECT's territory)

| Requirement | Point |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary protector** | Required where communications conductors enter a building — limits surge energy from the network |
| **Grounding** | The communications grounding system is bonded to the building's grounding electrode system |
| **Separation from power** | Communications cables must be kept physically separated from power conductors (a minimum separation or a permanent barrier), and must not share a raceway, box or cabinet with power conductors except where the Code explicitly permits |
| **Cable markings** | **CM** general, **CMR** riser, **CMP** plenum, **CMX** limited use; **CL2/CL3** for Class 2/3 circuits — a plenum space requires **CMP** |
| **Class 1/2/3 circuits** | Power-limited circuits with their own wiring rules; Class 2/3 are limited in energy, which is why they may use smaller conductors and different methods |
| **Article 810** | Radio and television antennas — lead-in conductors, masts, grounding |
| **Article 820** | Community antenna/CATV distribution |

**Where PEC ends and the electronics code begins:** PEC Chapter 8 governs the **safety of the wiring method** — separation, protection, grounding, cable type for the space. The **Philippine Electronics Code** governs the electronic systems themselves — radio, broadcast, telecommunications and electronic safety installations — under RA 9292.

## 5. Ethics

The **Code of Ethics** is adopted under §7(12) and violations are punishable under §35(9) — it is enforceable law, not etiquette. The five that are actually asked:

| Duty | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| **Public safety first** | The safety, health and welfare of the public override client or employer interest. Never sign off work you know is unsafe |
| **Competence** | Practise only within your legal scope and technical competence; refer beyond it |
| **Truthfulness** | Reports, tests and claims must be factual. No false advertising, no unverified performance claims |
| **Confidentiality** | Client and employer information is not disclosed without authority |
| **Conflict of interest** | Disclose any financial interest in equipment or contractors you recommend; do not use a position for personal gain |

Two more that appear in scenario items: **no aiding unlicensed practice** (employing or fronting for a non-registrant), and **no improper use of the seal** (sealing work you did not perform or supervise).

## 6. Worked legal scenarios

**S1.** A plant has 40 technicians and no engineer. The ECT prepares, signs and seals the layout drawings. Verdict?

> **Violation.** §5(c) confines the ECT to non-engineering work; sealing is reserved to the PECE by §5(b), and §35(d)/(f) penalise assuming the authority. The absence of an engineer is not a statutory exception — the only exemptions are the §26 foreign special permits.

**S2.** A registered ECE of 25 years signs and seals plans for a client. Verdict?

> **Violation.** 25 years of experience does not confer PECE authority; §5(b) makes sealing a matter of **category**, not seniority. Penalty under §35(g)/(i).

**S3.** A company offers electronics design services and argues its registration is valid because it employs three PECEs. Verdict?

> **The entity cannot be licensed.** §28 forbids registration of a firm, company, partnership or corporation for the practice; the registrants may practise collectively, but each answers for their own acts. Employment satisfies the sealing requirement, not the licensing one.

**S4.** An ECT installs a two-way radio system without an NTC authorisation, and the client's system operates unlicensed. Verdict?

> **§35(h)** penalises a registered professional who **maintains an unlicensed or unregistered communications system** or device. The criminal exposure is not only the client's.

**S5.** A technician candidate passed 68% in the single ECT subject. What happens?

> With **one subject**, 68% is below 70%, and the removal privilege presupposes a **majority of subjects passed** — with a single subject there is no majority of passes. The candidate has failed the examination. (For a multi-subject ECE exam, 68% in one subject with a majority passed would allow one removal.)

**S6.** A PECE seals a report prepared entirely by another PECE, without any indication of what he did. Verdict?

> **§35(f)** — a PECE may sign work prepared by another only if the document clearly indicates **the part actually performed**. Otherwise it is a penal act, and his own certificate is exposed under §23.

## 7. Traps

| Trap | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| "PEC" used for both codes | **Philippine Electrical Code** = electrical board; **Philippine Electronics Code** = electronics board |
| "The IIEE issues the electronics code" | The **Board of Electronics Engineering**, under RA 9292 §7(16)/§39 |
| Treating a code as a statute | It is a technical code under the IRR; **effective 15 days after publication** |
| Any ECE or the most senior person seals | **PECE only** (§5(b)) |
| An ECT seals because no engineer is available | No such exception; §35 penalises it |
| 70% general average passes | **70% in each subject** (§16) |
| Removal exam for any failure | Only if the **majority** was passed and the failed rating is **60–70%** |
| A company can be licensed if it employs registrants | **§28**: never — the licence is personal |
| Old RA 5734 registrants become PECE | They become **ECE** with the same licence number (§38) |
| Penalty under RA 8981 for unlicensed practice | Unlicensed practice is **RA 9292 §35** (₱100k–₱1M / 6 mo–6 yr); the heavier RA 8981 §15 penalty is for **examination fraud** |
| Board decisions are final on promulgation | Appealable to the **Commission within 15 days**; otherwise final and enforceable |
| A GFCI needs a ground wire | It compares outgoing and return current — it works without one |
| 3% voltage drop over the whole installation | **3% branch**, **5% total** (feeder + branch) |
| Breaker size set by the appliance | The breaker protects the **conductor**; the appliance needs its own protection |
