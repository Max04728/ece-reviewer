## Part A — RA 9292 and Regulation

### 1. RA 9292 Scope of Practice

**RA 9292 — Electronics Engineering Law of 2004**, approved April 17, 2004; it repealed RA 5734 (1969).
- **Sec. 1:** short title — "Electronics Engineering Law of 2004".
- **Sec. 2:** statement of policy — the State regulates electronics engineering to protect life, property and the public.
- **Sec. 3:** definition and interpretation of terms (electronics, electronics engineering, PECE, ECE, ECT).
- **Sec. 4 — Categories of Practice:** **PECE** (Professional Electronics Engineer), **ECE** (Electronics Engineer), **ECT** (Electronics Technician).
- **Sec. 5 — Nature and Scope of Practice:** design, construction, installation, operation, maintenance, repair and overhaul of electronic equipment and systems.
- Covered fields: radio and television, telecommunications, computers, semiconductors, instrumentation and control, industrial electronics, broadcast, and ICT.
- Practice also includes consultation, research and development, teaching, inspection, and the preparation of plans, specifications and reports.
- **Sec. 30 — Code of Ethics and Code of Technical Standards of Practice:** the Board adopts both; the technical standards = the **Philippine Electronics Code**.
- **Secs. 37–38:** transitory provision and vested rights for holders under RA 5734.
- **Sec. 39:** implementing rules and regulations.

**Conditions:** only the three RA 9292 categories may practise electronics engineering; the ECT scope is technician-level and is exercised under ECE or PECE supervision.

> [!tip] High-yield ★
> RA 9292 Sec. 4 lists the categories, Sec. 5 gives the scope. "Which section defines categories of practice?" → **Sec. 4**; "which section lists the scope?" → **Sec. 5**.

---

### 2. Titles: PECE, ECE, ECT and Seal

**Three protected titles ★** — no person may assume, use or advertise them without a valid certificate.
- **PECE — Professional Electronics Engineer:** the advanced category; requires registration plus documented professional experience and the PRC/BOECE requirements.
- **ECE — Electronics Engineer:** licensed by the PRC Board of Electronics Engineering after the ECE licensure examination.
- **ECT — Electronics Technician:** licensed technician category, examined separately.
- **Seal:** only the **PECE** may sign and seal electronics plans, designs and documents, and only while the certificate is valid.
- **Limits on the seal:** a PECE may not seal work prepared by another unless the document clearly shows the part he actually performed.

**Sec. 35 penalizes ★:** false statements to the Board; using another person's certificate, ID, APO card **or seal**; using a revoked or suspended certificate.
Also: practising or using a title without registration; stamping after revocation or suspension; signing work not actually performed.

**Symbols**

- PRC — Professional Regulation Commission, the government agency that regulates and supervises all the professions, including electronics engineering, dimensionless (agency name)
- BOECE — Board of Electronics Engineering (also BEE), the PRC regulatory board for the ECE profession that sets the registration requirements, dimensionless (board name)
- APO — Accredited Professional Organization, the integrated professional organization for electronics engineers (the IECEP under RA 9292 Sec. 32), whose membership card is one of the credentials whose misuse Sec. 35 penalizes, dimensionless (organization name)

**Conditions:** using any letter or word that conveys registered status while unregistered is itself the offense — intent to defraud is not an element to be proven.

> [!warning] Exam trap
> ECEs and ECTs do **not** sign and seal as PECEs do. Questions that offer "ECE may affix the seal" are wrong — the seal belongs to the PECE.

### 3. PRC and BEE Powers and Penal Provisions

**PRC** — Professional Regulation Commission, created by PD 223 and strengthened by RA 8981; it supervises all regulated professions.
**BEE / BOECE** — Board of Electronics Engineering, the regulatory board for the ECE profession under PRC supervision.

**RA 9292 sections to memorize ★**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Sec. 6 | Composition of the Board |
| Sec. 7 | Powers and functions of the Board |
| Sec. 8 | Qualifications of Board members |
| Sec. 9 | Term of office |
| Sec. 10 | Compensation and allowances |
| Sec. 17 | Release of examination results |
| Sec. 24 | Reinstatement of a revoked certificate |
| Sec. 30 | Code of Ethics and Code of Technical Standards |
| Sec. 31 | Continuing Professional Education |
| Sec. 32 | Integrated and Accredited Professional Organization (IECEP) |
| Sec. 33 | Foreign reciprocity |
| Sec. 35 | **Penal provision** |
| Sec. 36 | Assistance of law enforcement agencies |
| Sec. 39 | Implementing rules and regulations |

**Composition (Sec. 6):** a **Chairman and two members**, appointed by the President.
**Powers (Sec. 7):** administers oaths; issues, suspends and revokes certificates; investigates violations; prepares the licensure examinations; adopts the Code of Technical Standards.
**Qualifications (Sec. 8) ★:** every Board member must be a **PECE** with at least **10 years of active practice** in electronics engineering.
**Examination results (Sec. 17):** the Board must release the results within **15 days** after the examination.
**Reinstatement (Sec. 24):** a revoked certificate may be reinstated, on application and payment of the required fees, after **2 years**.

**Statutory definition of "electronics" (Sec. 3(a)) ★**

$$\text{electronics} = \text{science of devices using the flow of electrons in a vacuum, gas, plasma or semiconductor}$$

**Sec. 35 penalty ★:** fine of **₱100,000 to ₱1,000,000**, or imprisonment of **6 months to 6 years**, or both, at the court's discretion.
It also covers unregistered practice, sealing after revocation, and violation of the Act, the rules, the Code of Ethics or the Code of Technical Standards.

**Conditions:** the penalty applies to each enumerated act and to any violation of the Code of Ethics or Technical Standards; the court may impose fine, imprisonment, or both; the Sec. 8 ten-year requirement counts active practice in electronics engineering, the Sec. 17 period runs after the examination, and Sec. 24 reinstatement is discretionary and requires application plus payment of fees.

> [!tip] High-yield ★
> Penal provision = **RA 9292 Sec. 35**: ₱100,000–₱1,000,000 or 6 months–6 years, or both. Both halves are testable.

## Part B — Telecom and Data Laws

### 4. RA 7925 Telecom Policy and Carriers

**RA 7925 — Public Telecommunications Policy Act of the Philippines (1995).**
- **Policy:** a competitive, reliable and affordable telecommunications environment with **universal access** to basic telephone service.
- **Carrier categories:** public telecommunications entities (PTEs), carriers, and **value-added service (VAS) providers**.
- **Franchise rule:** a public telecommunications entity needs a **congressional franchise** plus a **CPCN from the NTC** before operating.
- **VAS rule:** a VAS provider does **not** need a franchise; it registers with the NTC and may lease network facilities.
- **Interconnection:** mandatory and non-discriminatory among authorized carriers, at reasonable rates.
- **Rate regulation:** tariff approval by the NTC; universal access and lifeline rate programs for underserved areas.
- **Tax:** public telecommunications entities are subject to the **3% franchise tax** on gross receipts, in lieu of other percentage taxes. **Note:** this is a **NIRC** imposition (franchise tax on telecommunications), **not** a provision of RA 7925 — the Act contains no tax section. Verify the current rate against the NIRC before relying on it.
- **Related laws:** RA 11202 (Mobile Number Portability, 2019) · RA 11934 (SIM Registration Act, 2022) · RA 10929 (Free Internet Access in Public Places) · RA 10844 (DICT, 2016).

**Symbols**

- CPCN — Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity, the NTC authorization that a public telecommunications entity must hold before serving the public, dimensionless (permit name)
- NTC — National Telecommunications Commission, the agency that regulates telecommunications services, issues the CPCN and registers VAS providers, dimensionless (agency name)
- DICT — Department of Information and Communications Technology, the department created by RA 10844 (2016) to which the NTC is attached, dimensionless (agency name)
- PTE — public telecommunications entity, a carrier offering public telecom service for compensation, which needs both a congressional franchise and a CPCN, dimensionless (carrier category name)
- VAS — value-added service, a service that enhances a carrier's network and needs only NTC registration and no congressional franchise, dimensionless (service category name)

**Conditions:** the franchise-plus-CPCN rule binds entities offering public telecom service for compensation; VAS providers need only NTC registration.

> [!warning] Exam trap
> A VAS provider needs **no congressional franchise** — only NTC registration. A public telecommunications entity needs **both** a franchise and a CPCN.

### 5. NTC Mandates and Regulations

**National Telecommunications Commission (NTC).**
- **Legal basis:** created by **EO 546 (July 23, 1979)**, reorganized by **EO 125 and EO 125-A (1987)**, and attached to the **DICT** (RA 10844, 2016).
- **Core mandates:** regulate telecom services, allocate the **radio spectrum**, enforce radio laws, promote competition and universal access.

**Permits and authorizations ★**

| Instrument | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Franchise (Congress) | authority to operate a public telecom service |
| CPCN | certificate of public convenience and necessity to serve the public |
| Provisional Authority (PA) | temporary authority pending CPCN decision |
| Certificate of Registration | registration of radio/telecom services and VAS |
| Radio Station License | authority to operate a radio station |
| Type Approval / Type Acceptance | certification of radio and telecom equipment |
| Spectrum User Fee | annual fee for assigned frequencies |

- **Enforcement powers:** inspection, suspension or revocation of permits, fines, closure of illegal operations, and confiscation of unlicensed equipment.
- **Consumer protection:** service standards, complaint handling, interconnection disputes, number portability (RA 11202) and SIM registration (RA 11934).
- **Public utilities rule:** telecom entities are public utilities; 60% Filipino ownership applies under the 1987 Constitution, Art. XII, Sec. 11.
- **Related issuances:** NTC Memorandum Circulars on type approval, spectrum use and service performance, alongside the Philippine Electronics Code for installations.

**Conditions:** transmitting without a frequency assignment or licence is criminal and administrative; equipment needs type approval before sale or import.

> [!tip] High-yield ★
> Order of the permit chain: **franchise → CPCN → radio station license / certificate of registration**. Names and sequence are the usual board item.

### 6. RA 10173 Data Privacy Act

**RA 10173 — Data Privacy Act of 2012**, effective September 2012; rules in the IRR (2016); enforced by the **National Privacy Commission (NPC)**.
- **Scope:** any processing of personal data of individuals in the Philippines, including offshore processing by Philippine entities.
- **Personal information:** any information from which identity is apparent or can be reasonably ascertained.
- **Sensitive personal information:** race, ethnic origin, marital status, age, color, religious or political affiliation, health, education, genetic or sexual life, offenses.
- **Principles:** transparency, legitimate purpose, proportionality.
- **Obligations ★:** appoint a **Data Protection Officer**; publish a privacy policy; register data processing systems; implement security measures; conduct impact assessments.
- **Breach notification ★:** notify the NPC and affected data subjects within **72 hours** of knowledge of a breach (NPC Circular No. 16-03).
- **Data subject rights:** informed, object, access, rectify, erase or block, damages, data portability, and complaint.
**Penalties (Secs. 25–30) ★**

| Offense | Imprisonment | Fine |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized processing, personal — Sec. 25(a) | 1–3 years | ₱500,000–₱2,000,000 |
| Unauthorized processing, sensitive — Sec. 25(b) | 3–6 years | ₱500,000–₱4,000,000 |
| Negligent access, personal — Sec. 26(a) | 1–3 years | ₱500,000–₱2,000,000 |
| Negligent access, sensitive — Sec. 26(b) | 3–6 years | ₱500,000–₱4,000,000 |
| Improper disposal, personal — Sec. 27(a) | 6 months–2 years | ₱100,000–₱500,000 |
| Improper disposal, sensitive — Sec. 27(b) | 1–3 years | ₱100,000–₱1,000,000 |
| Concealment of breach — Sec. 30 | 1.5–5 years | ₱500,000–₱1,000,000 |

**Symbols**

- IRR — Implementing Rules and Regulations, the rules issued to carry RA 10173 into effect (the Data Privacy Act IRR of 2016), dimensionless (rules instrument)

**Conditions:** consent is not the only lawful basis — contract, legal obligation, vital and legitimate interests also apply; the 72-hour clock runs from **knowledge**.

> [!warning] Exam trap
> The 72-hour notice goes to the **NPC and the affected data subjects** — never "within 30 days".

### 7. RA 10175 Cybercrime Prevention Act

**RA 10175 — Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012**, approved September 12, 2012.

**Sec. 4 offenses ★**

| Group | Offenses |
|---|---|
| (a) Offenses against confidentiality, integrity, availability | illegal access, illegal interception, data interference, system interference, misuse of devices |
| (b) Computer-related | forgery, fraud, identity theft |
| (c) Content-related | cybersex, child pornography, unsolicited commercial communications, **libel** |
| (d) Other | aiding, abetting or attempting any of the above |

- **Sec. 6 — one degree higher ★:** any crime under the Revised Penal Code committed through a computer system is punished **one degree higher** than the RPC penalty.
- **Sec. 8 — penalty floor ★:** a fine of **at least ₱200,000**, up to a maximum commensurate to the damage incurred, in addition to imprisonment.
- **Sec. 12:** real-time collection of traffic data by law enforcement.
- **Sec. 13 — data retention ★:** service providers must **preserve** traffic data and subscriber information for at least **6 months** from the date of the transaction.
- **Sec. 19:** DOJ power to order blocking or **takedown** of computer data.
- **Sec. 24:** creation of the **Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC)**; DOJ Office of Cybercrime handles investigation.
- **Cyber libel:** libel (RPC Art. 355) through ICT, one degree higher — the Supreme Court fixed a **12-year** prescription (Disini, 2014).

**Symbols**

- RPC — Revised Penal Code, the penal law whose crimes, when committed through a computer system, are punished one degree higher under Sec. 6, dimensionless (statute name)
- DOJ — Department of Justice, the department empowered under Sec. 19 to order the blocking or takedown of computer data, with its Office of Cybercrime handling investigation, dimensionless (agency name)
- RTC — Regional Trial Court, the trial court designated to hear cybercrime cases, dimensionless (court name)

**Conditions:** the one-degree-higher rule requires the RPC crime to be committed by, through or with ICT; jurisdiction is with the designated cybercrime RTC.

> [!tip] High-yield ★
> Remember three numbers: **one degree higher** (Sec. 6), **₱200,000 minimum fine** (Sec. 8), **6 months data preservation** (Sec. 13 — not Sec. 12, which is real-time traffic collection).

## Part C — Safety, Codes and Ethics

### 8. DOLE DO 13 OSHS Safety

**DOLE Department Order No. 13, s. 1998 — Occupational Safety and Health Standards (OSHS).**
Issued under **Article 162 of the Labor Code (PD 442)**; it applies to all workplaces and all employers.

**Key rules ★**

| Rule | Coverage |
|---|---|
| 1010 | definitions, scope and general provisions |
| 1020 | registration of establishments with DOLE |
| 1030 | training of personnel in OSH |
| 1040 | **Health and Safety Committee** |
| 1050 | notification and investigation of accidents |
| 1080 | personal protective equipment (PPE) |
| 1170 | electrical safety |
| 1180 | fire protection and firefighting facilities |
| 1960 | OSH in the construction industry |

- **Rule 1040:** every workplace must organize a Health and Safety Committee, which meets at least once a month; the committee includes management and worker representatives.
- **Safety officer:** required depending on the number of workers and the risk level; the safety officer implements the OSH program and maintains accident records.
- **Rule 1050:** work accidents and occupational illnesses must be reported to DOLE; fatal and serious accidents are investigated.
- **RA 11058 (2018)** strengthened compliance — it requires OSH personnel, safety committees, mandatory training and reporting, and raised penalties.
- **Penalty (RA 11058 Sec. 28(a)):** administrative fine of **not more than ₱100,000 per day** until the violation is corrected — a ceiling, with **no minimum floor**. Sec. 28(b) adds a separate fine of up to ₱100,000 for the acts it enumerates.
- Repeat violations double the fine; work stoppage or closure may also be ordered.

**Symbols**

- DOLE — Department of Labor and Employment, the department that issues the Occupational Safety and Health Standards (DO 13, s. 1998) and with which establishments register, dimensionless (agency name)
- OSH — occupational safety and health, the subject matter of DO 13 (the OSHS), dimensionless (field name)
- DO — Department Order, the form of issuance of DO 13, s. 1998, dimensionless (issuance type)

**Conditions:** the OSHS is a minimum standard — stricter industry rules and the electrical/electronics codes prevail; construction follows Rule 1960.

> [!warning] Exam trap
> Daily fines and the Health and Safety Committee's **monthly** meeting are the tested details. DO 13 is the OSHS; RA 11058 is the law that strengthened its penalties.

### 9. Philippine Electronics Code Books

**Philippine Electronics Code (PEC)** — the **Code of Technical Standards of Practice** mandated by **RA 9292 Sec. 30**, published by **IECEP**.
- **Four books (Books 1 to 4)** — the ECE board calls the set the "4-book Philippine Electronics Code".
- **Adoption:** BOECE **Resolution No. 12, Series of 2014** formally adopted the 4-book Code as a referral document to the **National Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096)**.
- **Book coverage:** Book 1 carries general provisions, definitions and administrative requirements.
- The technical books cover radio and broadcast, telecommunications and ICT/structured cabling, and electronics installation safety.
- **Cabling standards:** TIA/EIA-568 (cabling) · TIA-569 (pathways) · TIA-606 (labelling) · TIA-607 (grounding) · ISO/IEC 11801 (generic cabling).
- **Cabling facts:** permanent link 90 m · channel 100 m · two outlets per work area · 3 m slack at the telecom room.
- OFNP for plenum, OFNR for riser; never route through elevator shafts.
- **Related codes:** Philippine Electrical Code (PRC electrical law) · National Building Code (PD 1096) · Fire Code (RA 9514) · NTC type-approval circulars.

**PEC hazardous locations ★**

- **Class I Division 1:** flammable gases or vapours present continuously, intermittently or periodically under normal operation.
- **Required equipment:** intrinsically safe, explosion-proof or purged equipment, plus the specific wiring methods the Code prescribes.

**Symbols**

- PEC — Philippine Electronics Code, the Code of Technical Standards of Practice mandated by RA 9292 Sec. 30 and published by IECEP, dimensionless (code name)
- BOECE — Board of Electronics Engineering, the PRC board that adopted the 4-book Code through Resolution No. 12, Series of 2014 as a referral document to the National Building Code (PD 1096), dimensionless (board name)
- OFNP — optical fiber nonconductive plenum, the cable rating required for plenum (air-handling) spaces, dimensionless (cable rating)
- OFNR — optical fiber nonconductive riser, the cable rating used for riser (vertical shaft) runs, dimensionless (cable rating)

**Conditions:** as a referral document to the Building Code, compliance is enforced through building permits and occupancy, and through RA 9292 for practice; a Class I Division 1 area is judged hazardous during normal operation, so ordinary equipment and wiring methods are never acceptable there.

> [!warning] Exam trap
> Confirm the book titles against the **latest IECEP edition** before exam day.
> Reliably tested: **four books**, **BOECE Resolution No. 12, s. 2014**, and the **RA 9292 Sec. 30** mandate.

### 10. IECEP Code of Ethics

**IECEP — Institute of Electronics Engineers of the Philippines**, the **Integrated and Accredited Professional Organization (APO)** under **RA 9292 Sec. 32**; established 1950.
**RA 9292 Sec. 30** mandates two instruments: the **Code of Ethics** and the **Code of Technical Standards of Practice**.

**Fundamental canons ★**
- Hold paramount the **safety, health and welfare of the public** in all professional work.
- Practise only within the limits of personal **competence**; undertake work only when qualified.
- Issue statements and opinions only when **objective and truthful**; no false or misleading advertising.
- Act as a **faithful agent** of the client or employer, and disclose conflicts of interest.
- Avoid **conflicts of interest**; no solicitation or acceptance of improper inducements.
- Maintain **confidentiality** of client and employer information.
- Build professional reputation on **merit**, and give credit for the work of others.
- Report **unlicensed practice** and violations of the Code to the Board or IECEP.
- Sign and seal only work personally prepared or actually supervised.
- Support continuing professional education (RA 9292 Sec. 31) and the profession's technical standards.

**Sanctions:** violation of the Code of Ethics is expressly punishable under **RA 9292 Sec. 35**, and the Board may suspend or revoke the certificate of registration.

**Corporate social responsibility (CSR) ★** — a firm's obligation to stakeholders beyond profit, covering **employees**, the **community** and the **environment**.

$$\text{Triple bottom line} = \text{profit} + \text{people} + \text{planet}$$

**Conditions:** the Code binds all PECEs, ECEs and ECTs; the "public welfare first" canon overrides client instructions and employer convenience; CSR sits beyond minimum legal compliance, but the safety, health and environmental duties of the canons remain mandatory.

> [!tip] High-yield ★
> Ranking the canons: public safety first, then competence, then fidelity to the client. An item that pits confidentiality against public safety is answered by **public safety**.

## Part D — Intellectual Property

### 11. Intellectual Property

**RA 8293 — Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines ★** — one Code covering patents, utility models, industrial designs, trademarks, copyright and trade secrets.

**Forms of protection ★**

| Form | Subject matter | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Patent | an invention, product or process | new, useful and non-obvious, industrially applicable |
| Utility model | a minor mechanical or structural invention | new and industrially applicable; lower inventive step than a patent |
| Industrial design | the ornamental appearance of an article | new and ornamental |
| Trademark | a distinctive mark identifying the source of goods | distinctive; a service mark does the same for services |
| Copyright | literary and artistic works, including software | automatic on creation, no registration required |
| Trade secret | confidential commercial information | secret, commercially valuable, reasonable secrecy measures |

**General terms of protection ★**

| Form | Term |
|---|---|
| Patent | 20 years from the filing date |
| Utility model | 7 years from the filing date, with no renewal |
| Industrial design | 5 years from the filing date, renewable for two further periods of 5 years |
| Trademark | 10 years from registration, renewable for periods of 10 years |
| Copyright | the life of the author plus 50 years after death |
| Trade secret | as long as the information remains secret |

**Conditions:** patent, utility model, industrial design and trademark terms run from the filing or registration date while copyright runs from creation; IP rights are territorial, so Philippine registration protects only within the Philippines, and trade-secret protection has no fixed term but ends once the information becomes public.

> [!warning] Exam trap
> Copyright is **automatic on creation** — no registration is needed to own it. A patent, by contrast,
> is granted only after examination, and an invention that has lost novelty through prior public
> disclosure can no longer be patented.
